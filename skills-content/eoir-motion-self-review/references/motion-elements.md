# Motion Elements and Predictable Objections

Read the section matching the motion in front of you. Each section gives the governing authority, the elements that need their own argument subsection, and the objections OPLA or the Judge will reach for first.

Two standing cautions.

Cite-check nothing from memory. The authorities named below are the well-known anchors for each motion type and are given so you know what to look for in the draft, not so you can supply citations the draft is missing. If the draft omits an authority you would expect, say what proposition needs support and let the attorney find and verify the cite. If the draft cites something you cannot confirm, mark it VERIFY.

Regulations and precedent in this area move quickly, and A.G. and BIA decisions on termination and administrative closure have been overruled and reinstated more than once. If the currency of an authority matters to whether an item is blocking, say so and route it to VERIFY rather than asserting the current state of the law.

## 1. Motion to terminate

**Governing authority.** 8 C.F.R. 1003.18(d) sets out the grounds on which an Immigration Judge may terminate, including the discretionary ground at 1003.18(d)(1)(ii)(B) for a respondent prima facie eligible for naturalization, relief, or lawful status where USCIS has jurisdiction. 8 C.F.R. 1003.1(m) is the Board's counterpart. Matter of Coronado Acevedo addresses termination where it is necessary for the respondent to pursue relief before USCIS. Confirm from the draft which subsection it invokes; a motion that cites the regulation generally without naming a subsection has not identified its own ground.

**Elements needing their own subsection under 1003.18(d)(1)(ii)(B):**

1. Prima facie eligibility for naturalization, relief from removal, or lawful status. Where the path to status runs through a waiver, an exception, or a discretionary predicate, prima facie eligibility for that predicate is part of this element and belongs in this section, not in the fact statement.
2. USCIS jurisdiction to adjudicate if the respondent were not in proceedings.
3. Filing with USCIS, unless the motion relies on the provision dispensing with filing for a respondent prima facie eligible for adjustment or naturalization. If the draft relies on that provision, it should say so expressly.

Beyond the elements, a strong draft also addresses: why the Immigration Court cannot supply the same relief, why duplicating a substantially complete agency adjudication wastes hearing time, and why termination without prejudice leaves the Department's interests intact.

**Predictable objections.**

- Adjustment eligibility bars. For an adjustment-based motion, the draft should name the bars that could apply and dispose of them rather than leave them unmentioned. INA 245(c) is the usual field.
- Unlawful presence or overstay charged in the NTA, which the draft should address directly rather than route around.
- Speculation about whether USCIS will ever act. If the draft cannot say when a decision is expected, it should at least document diligence: interviews completed, requests for decision, expedite requests.
- Discretion. The regulation is discretionary, so equities need affirmative development. Family ties, criminal history or its absence, length of residence, manner of entry.
- Currency of the governing precedent, which has changed more than once. Route to VERIFY.

## 2. Motion to reopen

**Governing authority.** INA 240(c)(7); 8 C.F.R. 1003.23(b) for proceedings before the Immigration Judge, 1003.2(c) before the Board.

**Elements needing their own subsection:**

1. Timeliness. 90 days from the final administrative order, or an exception. This goes first and on the face of the motion.
2. Numerical limit. Generally one motion to reopen, or an applicable exception.
3. New evidence that is material and was not available and could not have been discovered or presented at the former hearing. Each of those adjectives is a separate showing.
4. Prima facie eligibility for the relief that reopening would allow the respondent to seek.
5. Where reopening is sought to apply for relief, the application itself and supporting documents submitted with the motion.

**Predictable objections.**

- Evidence that was available at the time of the hearing, which defeats the motion regardless of its strength.
- Changed personal circumstances presented as changed country conditions. The changed-country-conditions exception to the time and number limits requires a change in conditions in the country of removal, evidenced and compared to the conditions at the time of the prior hearing. A draft that argues the respondent's own situation changed has not met the exception.
- Failure to attach the underlying application, which alone supports denial.
- Discretion. Reopening is discretionary even when the showing is made, so equities need development.
- Departure from the United States, which raises the departure bar in some postures.

## 3. Motion to reconsider

**Governing authority.** INA 240(c)(6); 8 C.F.R. 1003.23(b)(1) and 1003.2(b).

**Elements needing their own subsection:**

1. Timeliness. 30 days from the final administrative order.
2. Numerical limit. Generally one.
3. An error of law or fact in the prior decision, identified specifically with the reasoning that shows it was error, or a change in law since the decision.

**Predictable objections.**

- A motion to reconsider used to submit new evidence. That is a motion to reopen. Mixing them is the most common defect: if the draft attaches new evidence and calls itself a motion to reconsider, flag the mismatch and say which motion the facts support.
- Reargument of points already decided without identifying an error, which is the most common substantive ground for denial.
- Failure to engage the actual reasoning of the prior decision.

## 4. Rescind an in absentia order

**Governing authority.** INA 240(b)(5)(C); 8 C.F.R. 1003.23(b)(4)(ii).

**Two distinct grounds, with different clocks, and the draft must pick one and argue it as such:**

- **Exceptional circumstances.** 180 days from the order. Requires exceptional circumstances beyond the respondent's control, supported by evidence: medical records, accident documentation, a declaration. Requires a declaration from the respondent, and where counsel error is alleged, compliance with the Lozada framework, which the draft should address expressly.
- **Lack of notice.** No time limit. Requires showing the respondent did not receive notice as required by statute. Address the presumption of delivery that attaches to the method of service used, and the address of record and any change of address filed.

**Predictable objections.**

- Arguing both grounds without separating them, so neither is cleanly established.
- Asserting non-receipt without a declaration or without addressing the delivery presumption.
- Failing to address whether the respondent kept the address of record current.
- Omitting the underlying application for relief the respondent would pursue if the order were rescinded.

## 5. Motion to continue

**Governing authority.** 8 C.F.R. 1003.29, good cause. The BIA's factor framework for continuances pending collateral relief has been revised repeatedly, so identify what the draft relies on and route currency to VERIFY.

**What the draft must establish:**

1. Good cause, stated specifically. Not "counsel needs more time" but what needs to happen, why it cannot happen before the current date, and what it will accomplish.
2. The likelihood the collateral matter will resolve favorably, where the continuance is sought pending a petition or application elsewhere.
3. Diligence to date, which is what distinguishes a first request from a pattern.
4. Whether DHS opposes. An unopposed continuance is a materially different motion and should say so on its face.
5. A proposed length or a date certain, rather than an open-ended request.

**Predictable objections.** Repeat requests without new circumstances; a collateral matter too speculative or too far from adjudication; lack of diligence; no proposed end date.

## 6. Motion to change venue

**Governing authority.** 8 C.F.R. 1003.20(b), good cause.

**What the draft must establish:**

1. Good cause, addressed through the recognized factors: administrative convenience, expeditious treatment, location of witnesses, cost and inconvenience to the parties, and where the respondent actually lives.
2. Concession of the factual allegations and the charge, or pleadings entered. Courts routinely expect pleadings before venue changes, and a motion that does not address this invites denial on that ground alone.
3. A fixed proposed destination court, with the reason it is the right one.
4. Documentation of the new address, and confirmation that a change of address was filed.

**Predictable objections.** No pleadings entered; no documentation of residence; a destination that does not follow from the stated reasons; DHS prejudice from witness location.

## 7. Motion to sever

**What the draft must establish:** which respondents are to be severed and why joint proceedings prejudice one of them; that the relief sought differs across respondents or that one respondent's case would be delayed by another's; and that severance serves efficiency rather than delay.

**Predictable objections.** Severance sought to obtain delay; overlapping evidence making separate hearings duplicative; no articulated prejudice.

---

## 8. Motion to suppress

**What the draft must establish:** the specific evidence to be suppressed, identified item by item; the constitutional or regulatory violation, with the recognized standard for exclusion in immigration proceedings, which is narrower than in criminal cases and generally requires an egregious violation or a regulatory violation prejudicing a protected interest; the factual basis by declaration or testimony; and the causal link between the violation and the evidence.

**Predictable objections.** Applying a criminal-law suppression standard without addressing the narrower immigration standard; a conclusory declaration; failure to identify the specific evidence; independent sources for the same evidence.

## 9. Motion for administrative closure

**Governing authority.** 8 C.F.R. 1003.18 and 1003.1(l). This is the most volatile area covered in this file. The availability of administrative closure and the governing framework have been overruled, restored, and revised by successive A.G. decisions and rulemaking. Identify what the draft relies on, and route the currency question to VERIFY rather than asserting the present state of the law.

**What the draft must establish:** the authority relied on for closure, with its currency addressed; the reason for closure, usually a collateral matter pending elsewhere; the anticipated timeline; DHS's position; and why closure rather than continuance or termination is the right disposition.

**Predictable objections.** DHS opposition, which carries substantial weight in most frameworks; no defined endpoint; a collateral matter too speculative.

## 10. Checks that apply to every motion

Run these regardless of motion type.

- **Relief requested is specific and matches the proposed order.** The prayer should name the disposition, whether it is with or without prejudice where that applies, the timing sought, and what the ruling should address. Then confirm the proposed order actually offers that disposition.
- **Every element has its own captioned subsection.** An element addressed only in the introduction and the conclusion is an element the Judge can find unaddressed.
- **Every factual assertion in the argument carries a tab citation or a specific dated event.** List the assertions that float.
- **Foreseeable bars named and disposed of**, even the ones that do not apply.
- **Government interests addressed**, not left to the response.
- **Adverse facts stated plainly.** Contrary allegations in the NTA, gaps in the record, unfavorable history. A motion that omits them loses credibility when OPLA supplies them.
- **Discretion developed** wherever the relief is discretionary, which is most of this list.
- **Oral statements by agency officials documented** somewhere in the record, or flagged as resting on counsel's recollection alone.
- **Each cited authority actually helps.** Check what the tribunal did, not only what it said. A decision that reversed a grant of the same relief, or resolved a jurisdictional question against the motion's position, is a liability even when the quoted sentence is favorable.
- **No quotation truncated at a point that hides a limitation.** Read the sentence after the quoted passage. Regulations in this area carry exceptions and carve-outs in the sentences immediately following the operative text.
