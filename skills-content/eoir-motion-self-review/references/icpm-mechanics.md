# ICPM Mechanics Checklist

Procedural and formal requirements for EOIR motions practice. These checks are objective, which is what makes them worth running every time: unlike a judgment call about argument strength, either the caption matches across pages or it does not.

A caution that applies to this whole file. The Immigration Court Practice Manual is revised periodically and local operating procedures vary by court. Treat the deadline figures below as the framework to reason with, not as verified current law. When a deadline determines whether an item is blocking, state the figure you are applying and tell the attorney to confirm it against the current ICPM edition and any standing order from the assigned Judge. A confidently wrong deadline is worse than a flagged unknown.

## 1. Filing deadlines

**General pre-hearing filings.** The ICPM sets a minimum lead time before the hearing for filings on which the party wants a ruling at that hearing. The figures customarily applied are 15 days in non-detained cases and 10 days in detained cases. Confirm both against the current manual.

**Response time.** The opposing party generally has 10 days to respond, extended when service was by mail. This matters for the review because a motion filed too close to the hearing may not leave a lawful response window even if it clears the party's own filing deadline. If the arithmetic does not leave OPLA its response time before the hearing date, say so.

**Motion-specific statutory clocks.** These override the general rule and are non-waivable in most postures:

| Motion | Window | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Motion to reopen | 90 days from the final administrative order | INA 240(c)(7)(C)(i) |
| Motion to reconsider | 30 days from the final administrative order | INA 240(c)(6)(B) |
| Rescind in absentia order, exceptional circumstances | 180 days from the order | INA 240(b)(5)(C)(i) |
| Rescind in absentia order, lack of notice | No time limit | INA 240(b)(5)(C)(ii) |

Numerical limits also apply: generally one motion to reopen and one motion to reconsider. Recognized exceptions exist, including for changed country conditions in an asylum-based motion to reopen and for certain joint motions. If the draft is a second motion of either type and does not address the numerical bar on its face, that is blocking.

**What to do when the draft does not give you what you need.** The deadline check requires the filing date, the next hearing date, and the detention status. If the draft omits any of them, do not guess. State which input is missing, state the deadline that would apply under each plausible answer, and put the missing input in VERIFY.

**How to write the finding.** Show the arithmetic. "Filed June 16, 2026 for a June 22, 2026 hearing. Six days. The non-detained pre-hearing minimum customarily applied is 15 days." Then give the fix: move the filing date, add a request for leave to file out of time with a good-cause showing, or identify the exception relied on.

## 2. Cover page

Check for each of these and flag any that is absent:

- Counsel block: attorney name with Esq., firm name, street address, city, state, zip, office phone, fax, email. EOIR ID where the firm uses one.
- Detention status flag, conventionally upper right in caps: NON-DETAINED or DETAINED.
- Court header, three lines: UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE / EXECUTIVE OFFICE FOR IMMIGRATION REVIEW / IMMIGRATION COURT with the city and state.
- Caption: respondent name in caps, "Respondent" designation, File No. with the A-number, "In Removal Proceedings."
- Assigned Immigration Judge by name.
- Next hearing date.
- Title of the pleading, centered, in caps, naming the relief sought precisely. "Respondent's Motion to Terminate Removal Proceedings Without Prejudice" is precise; "Motion to Terminate" is not, because without prejudice is part of the relief.

The Judge and hearing date are worth their own attention. A cover page that omits the hearing date makes the timeliness of the filing invisible to the clerk and to the reviewer, and it is the single most common cover-page omission.

## 3. Caption consistency

The caption appears on the motion, the exhibit index, the proposed order, and often the certificate of service. Compare them character by character. Common drift:

- Delimiter changes between pages: section symbols on one page, parentheses on another.
- Respondent name rendered differently: last-comma-first on one page, full legal name with middle initials on another.
- A-number formatting drift: with and without the letter A, with and without hyphens, spaces substituted for hyphens.
- Court line abbreviated on one page and spelled out on another.

Pick the rendering used on the motion cover as the reference and list every deviation with its location. This looks pedantic and is not: an inconsistent A-number is how a filing gets associated with the wrong record.

## 4. Body structure and pagination

- Roman-numeral top-level sections, lettered subsections, arabic sub-subsections. Confirm the sequence is complete with no skipped numeral. A jump from III to V means a section was cut and the numbering was not repaired.
- Section titles descriptive rather than bare. "Element Two: USCIS Has Jurisdiction to Adjudicate the Applications" tells the Judge what the section establishes; "Argument" does not.
- Pagination continuous from the first page of the brief through the exhibits and the certificate of service. Confirm no gaps and no duplicated numbers.
- Every page accounted for. If the packet has 57 numbered pages, all 57 should appear somewhere in the index or the brief.
- Tab divider sheets conventionally unnumbered, with the numbering carried by the substantive pages. Whatever convention the packet uses, it must be applied consistently.

## 5. Exhibit index and tab practice

- A separately captioned index page, titled to identify the filing it supports.
- Three columns: TAB, DESCRIPTION, PAGES.
- Descriptions that state both what the document is and what it establishes. "Form I-94, Arrival/Departure Record. Establishes inspection and admission at Houston, Texas, on November 6, 2016, as required by INA 245(a)" is a working description. "I-94" is a label. Working descriptions are what let a Judge find the fact without reading the exhibit.
- Sequential tab letters with none skipped.
- One document, or one tightly related set, per tab. Flag any tab bundling unrelated documents, since it forces imprecise record citation.
- Every physical component of the packet listed, including the proposed order and the certificate of service. The proposed order is the item most often omitted from the index.
- Page ranges reconciled against the actual packet, exactly. An index range that is off by one is a defect the reviewer will find in ten seconds.
- Unpublished circuit opinions attached as a tab and cited with the local rule permitting their use.

---

## 6. Signature block

- Attorney name with Esq., state bar number, firm name, full address, phone, fax, email.
- Signature line present.
- Date of signature consistent with the filing date and with the certificate of service date.
- Every contact detail identical to the cover page and to any firm letterhead appearing in the exhibits. Fax and phone numbers drift when a firm changes carriers and old letterhead stays in the tab set. Compare all three sources.

---

## 7. Proposed order

- Present. A motion without a proposed order asks the Judge to draft one.
- Caption matching the motion.
- Title identifying it as an order of the Immigration Judge.
- Disposition options, conventionally GRANTED and DENIED checkboxes, tracking the relief actually requested. If the motion asks for termination without prejudice, the GRANTED line should say without prejudice.
- A reasons line, and date and signature lines for the Judge.
- Listed in the exhibit index with its page number.

---

## 8. Certificate of service

- Present. Its absence is blocking.
- Names the specific pleading served. Generic boilerplate such as "this Written Pleadings" is a defect: it fails to establish what was served, and it signals the certificate was carried over from another filing without editing.
- States the date of service, which should reconcile with the filing date.
- Identifies the method: ECAS, mail, hand delivery, or electronic service.
- Identifies the served party and address: the correct OPLA office for the court, with the full address.
- Signature line and date.

---

## 9. Translations

Any foreign-language document in the packet needs an English translation and a certificate of translation from the translator, attesting competence in both languages and to the accuracy and completeness of the translation. Check that every foreign-language exhibit has both, and that the translation is tabbed with its source document rather than filed loose.

---

## 10. Length aids

A table of contents and a table of authorities are not required for every motion, but a motion running past roughly ten pages or citing more than about ten authorities is easier to grant with them. Treat their absence on a long, citation-dense motion as a SHOULD-FIX or OPTIONAL item depending on length, not as blocking.
