# Declaration Draft (Concise, Future-Persecution Focus)

Turn what the client actually told the firm into a clear, credible, first-person declaration, kept short and centered on the FORWARD-LOOKING fear of persecution. This is a draft for attorney and client review, not a final filing and not a document the client has yet adopted.

## The non-negotiable rule

Draft only from facts the client or attorney has provided. Never invent, embellish, assume, or fill in facts, dates, places, names, or events. A declaration is sworn testimony that anchors credibility under the REAL ID Act; one fabricated or guessed detail can sink the case and expose the client and the attorney. Where the narrative has a gap, a missing date, or an ambiguity, insert a bracketed flag such as [CLARIFY WITH CLIENT: approximate date of the second threat?] rather than guessing. Flagged gaps are the correct output; invented facts are never acceptable.

## Two hard constraints for this version

1. Six pages maximum. The finished declaration must fit in 6 pages or fewer in house format. Be concise. Cut repetition, background that does not carry an element, and any detail that does not build credibility or the fear of return. If the client's account cannot fit, keep the strongest, most probative facts and flag the rest for the attorney to decide.

2. Center future persecution. The organizing spine of the declaration is what the client fears will happen on return and why that fear is well founded. Past harm is included, but as the foundation that makes the future fear credible and specific, not as the main event. Every past fact earns its place by supporting the forward-looking claim.

## Before anything

Read PRACTICE_PROFILE.md for house style (declaration tone and format, first-person conventions, signature and interpreter blocks) and the work-product header. Note which relief is in play (from claim-evaluation if it has been run) so the narrative covers the elements that relief requires.

## What the short declaration must cover

Organize the client's own account so it establishes each element without argument (argument belongs in the brief). Keep each section tight.

- Identity and the protected characteristic. Who the client is and the characteristic tied to the protected ground (ethnicity, religion, political activity, group membership, and so on). Only the background that connects to the fear of return.
- Concise chronology of past harm. What happened, in order, in concrete detail the client actually recalls: approximate dates, places, who did what, what was said, injuries, threats. Include only what supports the future-persecution claim; compress or omit the rest.
- Nexus in the client's own words. Why the client believes they were and will be targeted (the persecutor's statements, insignia, ongoing pattern), so the on-account-of link emerges from the facts.
- Fear of return (the heart of this declaration). What the client believes will happen if returned, who would harm them, why the client believes the threat is current and specific, and any facts showing the persecutor still has interest, reach, or capacity. Give this the most space.
- Why the government will not protect them and why relocation is not safe. The client's own experience of reporting to authorities (or why they could not), and why moving elsewhere in the country would not remove the danger. This directly supports a well-founded fear.
- Timeliness facts (asylum). If the one-year deadline is an issue, the real facts bearing on a changed- or extraordinary-circumstances exception. Keep to facts only.

## Consistency check

Before finishing, cross-check the draft against any I-589, prior statement, or intake already in the record. Surface every inconsistency to the attorney in a short note at the end: dates that do not line up, events in a different order, names spelled differently. Do not silently correct them; the attorney resolves them with the client.

## Output format

Under the work-product header, produce:

1. The declaration in house style: numbered paragraphs, first person, past tense for events, present or future tense for the fear of return. Order: identity and protected characteristic, concise chronology, nexus, fear of return (longest section), protection and relocation, timeliness if relevant. Keep it to 6 pages or fewer.
2. Signature block, and where the client's language requires it, an interpreter or translation certification block (the declarant read it in a language they understand, or it was read back through a competent interpreter).
3. Flags for the attorney: a bulleted list of every [CLARIFY] item, every consistency issue, every place corroboration should match the narrative, and any strong facts that were cut for length so the attorney can decide whether to restore them.

## Guardrails

- Draft, not filing. The client must review, correct, and adopt it; the attorney supervises.
- No invented or embellished facts, ever. Flag gaps; never fill them.
- Do not coach the client toward a better story or suggest facts that would strengthen an element. Articulate what is there; identify what is missing.
- Keep the client's voice; avoid legal conclusions and argument in the declaration itself.
- Respect heightened confidentiality and the client's safety; this document names people and places that can endanger the client and others.
