# Part 1: Preliminary Security Questions (verbatim firm standard)

These 19 questions appear identically, in this order, word for word, in every asylum interview question set the firm produces. Reproduce them exactly. Do not paraphrase, reorder, trim, or "improve" them.

Several of these are, judged purely as drafting, compound and vague. Question 11 and question 12 both back-reference an entire preceding list rather than naming one act. That is intentional and correct here: the instrument predicts how the officer actually asks, and the officer asks in exactly this compressed form. A client prepared on a cleaned-up version of the question is a client surprised by the real one. This is why the craft rules elsewhere in this skill do not apply to this block.

Question 14 carries the qualifier "even if against your will." That phrasing tracks the absence of a duress exception in the material support bar and is the most consequential wording in the block. It must survive any edit.

---

1. Have you traveled or lived outside of the United States?
2. What countries have you traveled to and when?
3. Have you received immigration status in any other country?
4. Have you ever served in the military in any country?
5. Have you ever been detained or jailed anywhere?
6. Have you ever harmed anyone?
7. Have you ever committed a crime anywhere in the world?
8. Have you ever been accused or arrested for a crime anywhere in the world?
9. Have you ever been convicted of a crime anywhere?
10. Have you ever been involved in or accused of terrorist activity?
11. Have you ever helped someone do any of these things?
12. Have you ever threatened to do any of these things?
13. Are you or have you ever been a member of a terrorist group?
14. Have you ever provided support—such as food, money, or information—to anyone involved in violence or terrorist activity, even if against your will?
15. Have you ever solicited or recruited anyone for terrorist activity?
16. Have you ever received military training?
17. Has your spouse ever been involved in terrorist activity?
18. Have your parents ever been involved in terrorist activity?
19. Do you plan on doing anything illegal or harmful to the United States?

---

## Note for the attorney

This block is reproduced from the firm's existing templates, where it appears identically in every version. It was not drafted against a current USCIS source. Two things follow.

First, the block screens for the statutory bars, and an affirmative answer to several of these is case-dispositive. Where the client's history touches any of questions 4 through 18, that is an escalation to the supervising attorney before the client is prepped, not a drafting question.

Second, if the firm ever revisits this block, do it as a deliberate template change reviewed against current law, not as an incidental edit inside one client's document. Silent divergence between client files is worse than a flawed but consistent block.
