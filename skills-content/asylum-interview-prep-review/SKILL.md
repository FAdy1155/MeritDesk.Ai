# Asylum Interview Prep: Review

Do the last careful read of a drafted interview question set and hand the attorney a report they can act on. This is **attorney work product and a QA gate** before the document reaches the client.

Be demanding. A comment withheld here is a client walking into an interview unprepared on a question the officer was always going to ask.

But do not pad. A report where everything is blocking tells the supervising attorney nothing about what to fix first, and a reviewer who flags eighteen items to look thorough has buried the two that mattered.

## Before anything

Read `PRACTICE_PROFILE.md` for house style, escalation rules, and the work-product header.

Read `references/house-format.md` for the standard being enforced and `references/security-block.md` for the verbatim Part 1.

## Establish four things first

Ask if you have not been told, and do not begin until you have answers. Several checks are impossible or actively misleading without them.

1. **Draft type.** A generic reusable template, or a draft adapted to a specific client? Several rules invert between the two.
2. **Do you have the client record?** The I-589, the declaration, witness statements.
3. **Is this an affirmative Asylum Office case?** An EOIR defensive matter needs `direct-exam` reviewed against a different standard.
4. **Is the firm using a two-version format,** meaning a counsel version and a separate client practice guide? If so, plain-language answer notes belong in the client version and are not style defects there.

## Rules governing your own review

**The unverified rule, which overrides everything below.** Where a check depends on information you were not given, do not guess in either direction and do not skip it silently. Put it under UNVERIFIED and state exactly what you would need. This applies to every rule in this skill whether or not the rule says so. An unperformed check is not a passed check, and a report that quietly omits what it could not test reads as a clean bill of health.

**The fix rule.** Give a concrete fix for every finding, except where supplying one would require inventing a fact or doing research outside the record. There, say so in one line and name what the attorney must supply.

**Do not double-report.** One defect, one finding, citing both rules it violates. The coverage tables are an index, not findings; listing a gap there and again under BLOCKING is not duplication.

**Never fabricate a fact or a citation** to support a finding.

## A. Architecture

**A1. Orientation section** before Part 1 covering oath, interpreter logistics, right to correct a mistranslation, presence of counsel, biometrics, and that "I do not know" is a truthful answer where true. Absent entirely: SHOULD-FIX. Present but silent on who supplies the interpreter: SHOULD-FIX, because that omission can cost the interview date. [ADDED, not a historical firm convention.]

**A2. Part 1 is the verbatim security block.** Compare against `references/security-block.md`. Any rewording, omission, or reordering is BLOCKING. Confirm the count is 19 and that question 14 retains "even if against your will," which is the most consequential wording in the block.

**A3. Part 2 Travel History** must establish departure date, countries transited, protection sought or received in any of them and why not, date of entry into the United States, manner and place of entry, visa type, return trips since departure, and travel outside the US since arrival. A travel section covering only trips abroad, without entry into the United States, is incomplete however correctly it is titled. This is the firm's most-repeated template gap. Any missing item: BLOCKING.

**A4. Part 3 Asylum Application Review** must establish review of the I-589, its accuracy, corrections and the client's agreement with each, submission of a declaration, who assisted in preparation, document submission and genuineness, and documents that should have been submitted and were not. Any missing item: BLOCKING.

**A5. Final part is Closing Questions** with all three functions. A missing comprehension check is BLOCKING. Either other missing item is SHOULD-FIX.

**A6. Ordering, applied to the middle parts only,** meaning everything between Application Review and Closing Questions. The sequence runs: identity and group membership, then what happened, then who did it, then why the state will not help, then why the client cannot relocate, then what is feared on return. Walk the middle parts against that sequence and flag any out of position, whatever its subject. The protected ground appearing after the persecutor sections is the firm's known template defect, but do not limit the check to it.

Parts 1 through 3 and the closing part are fixed by convention and are not ordering defects. Parts that sit outside the sequence entirely, meaning family composition, nationality, per-theory parts, life in the United States, and the spouse and derivative parts, are also not ordering defects wherever they appear. Judge only the six sequenced stages against each other. Misordering: SHOULD-FIX.

## B. The security block is exempt from the craft rules

Part 1 is reproduced verbatim by firm convention, and several of its questions are compound and vague. Do not flag them under section D. The exemption follows the block itself rather than its position, so it applies even if the block has been moved; the move is separately blocking under A2, but do not also re-flag the block's compound questions.

If you believe a security block question is itself poorly drafted, raise it once under OPTIONAL as a suggestion that the firm revisit its template, and say plainly that the fix is a template change rather than a change to this draft.

Application Review questions are reviewable, except that confirmation-form questions there are correct by function and are not leading-question defects.

## C. Coverage

Produce two tables naming the part that covers each item, then carry every gap into the severity groups.

### C1. Required. Any of these missing is BLOCKING.

- statutory bars
- date and manner of entry into the United States
- firm resettlement: status or protection sought or received in any transit or third country, and, where the client is stateless or holds a contested nationality, whether there is any country where the client has a right to citizenship they have not pursued
- **the one-year filing deadline,** measured from the client's **last arrival** in the United States rather than first entry, and any changed or extraordinary circumstances. A draft that establishes only first entry, in a case with later arrivals, is defective even though it appears to cover the topic. [ADDED, but blocking: for a client who filed well after arrival this is the likeliest single point of failure at interview.]
- application accuracy, corrections, and who assisted
- the protected ground, tested rather than assumed: the client must be asked to explain the belief or identity, the practice, and their own role and frequency of participation
- past persecution, incident by incident
- nexus: why this actor would target this client
- the state being unwilling or unable to protect, wherever any feared persecutor is not the state. No such section at all is a blocking omission in its own right, not merely a thin one
- internal relocation
- well-founded fear: severity, likelihood, timing
- how the persecutors would learn the client had returned, and whether anyone is currently looking for the client
- separate coverage for every derivative applicant

### C2. Recommended. Absent from prior firm templates. Missing: SHOULD-FIX.

- prior statements the officer will hold the client to: visa application and embassy interview, port of entry or CBP statements, any credible or reasonable fear interview, any prior immigration filing
- US-side history: overstay, arrests in the United States, prior removal
- torture and government acquiescence, asked separately from general fear
- corroboration: evidence reasonably available but not obtained, and why
- mixed motive, and grounds imputed to the client that the client does not hold
- for any PSG-based theory: social recognition, whether people in that society would know who belongs, and immutability
- family composition, including where relatives currently live
- life in the United States

### C3. Template branch

If the draft is a generic template, a required item that can only be filled from client facts is satisfied by a labeled placeholder plus a drafting instruction. Absence of any such placeholder is still BLOCKING; a placeholder rather than content is not a defect.

## D. Craft defects. Default tier SHOULD-FIX unless stated.

**D1. Leading questions.** Three forms, and the third is the one that survives drafting: the question embeds its answer; the question invites confirmation, ending in "correct?" or "right?"; or the question **presupposes** the conclusion it should elicit. "Why couldn't you relocate to another area?" presupposes that the client could not. Ask whether there is anywhere they could live safely, then why not.

BLOCKING where the presupposed proposition is an element the client must establish in their own words, meaning harm, motive, fear, inability to relocate, or state inaction. SHOULD-FIX elsewhere. Confirmation-form questions inside Application Review are not defects.

**D2. Unsupported specifics.** Any question asserting a date, name, place, quotation, or event.

- *Adapted draft, record available:* trace each. Untraceable is BLOCKING, quoted. No fix required, since supplying one would mean inventing a fact; state what the attorney must confirm.
- *Adapted draft, no record:* UNVERIFIED, listed.
- *Generic template:* do not flag placeholders. Run the inverse check, which is the real risk in a reusable document. Flag client-identifying detail left baked in: named persons, specific incidents, specific cities, specific family facts, a particular country. BLOCKING, and here a fix **is** required, namely the placeholder that should replace it. Do not flag the claim type itself; a template scoped to religious persecution claims is correctly scoped, and its substantive knowledge questions are a required feature, not a leak.

**D3. Compound questions** seeking two facts at once. Split them.

**D4. Legal terminology** in client-facing text. Rewrite in plain language.

**D5. Missing escalation ladder.** A topic that jumps straight to narrative without the screening, count, timing, and actor steps.

**D6. Incidents blended together.** Every discrete incident needs its own labeled block covering when, where, who was present, who else was involved, what was said or done, what happened after, whether it was reported, and what the authorities did. Audit the completeness of every block present. Whether an incident in the record is missing entirely requires the record; without it, UNVERIFIED.

**D7. Distinct persecution theories blended** into one general part. BLOCKING where blending loses an element the other theory does not supply.

**D8. Persecutors not separately examined.** Each feared actor needs its own why-this-client drill-down. Flag any actor named once and dropped.

**D9. Missing exhaustion sweeps** after an enumerated list of actors or motives. An officer would often not ask these, so do not treat them as padding: they exist so the client does not leave a basis unstated and raise it later, which is where adverse credibility findings come from.

**D10. Internal relocation left generic.** The draft must name the specific cities or regions the officer will propose and close each. On a template, flag the absence of a placeholder instructing the drafter to do so.

**D11. Protection left on paper.** Asks whether a protective law exists but never whether it is enforced in practice.

**D12. Ongoing search not covered.** Where the record indicates anyone is currently looking for the client, ask how the client knows, what was said, and when contact last occurred. Recency is what makes the fear current.

**D13. Relocation history not covered.** Beyond naming cities, ask whether the client ever actually relocated, what happened, and why each feared actor could find them anywhere.

**D14. Third-country protection not closed out.** Asking whether protection was sought in transit countries without asking **why it was not** is incomplete.

**D15. Fear of return not individuated.** Harm feared must be asked separately for the client and for each family member by name, not once collectively.

**D16. Record anchors missing.** Questions testing a specific assertion should name the source. Requires the record; without it, UNVERIFIED. Skip entirely on a generic template. Flag separately any fact drawn from another declarant's statement that the client is never prepared to answer on.

**D17. Cross-document inconsistency.** Requires the record; without it, UNVERIFIED. Compare the draft against the I-589 and declarations and report every date, name, count, or sequence that does not match. Report the conflict; do not resolve it and do not propose which version is correct. The only line you add is what the attorney must confirm with the client. BLOCKING.

**D18. Country conditions currency.** Flag any question assuming a state of affairs in the country of feared harm that may have changed. Do not research it. File under UNVERIFIED and name what the attorney must confirm.

**D19. Record gap flags are not defects.** A question carrying an explicit `[RECORD GAP - confirm with client before use]` flag is the drafter correctly disclosing an unsupported point. Do not flag it under D2's unsupported-specifics branch and do not flag it as commentary under section E. A flag does not exempt the question from D2's template-leakage branch: a real client name inside a flagged question is still leakage. Report the count and confirm the flags are collected in a list at the end. An uncollected flag: SHOULD-FIX.

## E. Style. Default tier OPTIONAL; escalate to SHOULD-FIX where the defect impairs use.

Second person, direct address. Courtesy title for the spouse, first name for adult derivatives; skip if the draft names no one. One question per line. Parts formatted `Part N: Title`. Sub-groupings introduced by a short label ending in a colon.

The question set body carries questions only: no answer notes, commentary, or citations. Two exceptions, neither a defect: bracketed record-gap flags, and, in a generic template, the bracketed placeholders and drafting instructions C3 and D10 affirmatively require. If the firm uses the two-version format, answer notes in the client practice guide are expected.

## Report format

ALWAYS produce, under the work-product header:

1. **Draft snapshot.** Client or template, country, claim type, venue, the documents you reviewed, and anything not provided.
2. **The two coverage tables** from section C.
3. **BLOCKING.** Cannot go to the client as written.
4. **SHOULD-FIX.** Will draw an officer question the client is not ready for, or reads as sloppy.
5. **UNVERIFIED.** Every check you could not perform, and exactly what you would need. Never leave a check silently undone.
6. **OPTIONAL.** Improvements, not defects.
7. **Escalation flags.** Anything touching the security block, criminal history, or the bars, routed to the attorney per the profile.
8. **Verdict,** one of three:
   - **PASS** - no blocking findings and no required item sitting in UNVERIFIED.
   - **FAIL** - one or more blocking findings.
   - **CANNOT CERTIFY** - no blocking findings, but at least one required check landed in UNVERIFIED. Name what would convert it.

   State the recommended gaps separately from the verdict, so the attorney can weigh them as departures from the house standard rather than defects in it.
9. **The officer questions this draft does not prepare the client for.** List the ones you actually found. Do not pad to a fixed number.
10. **Prioritized edit checklist.** Blocking, then should-fix, then optional, so the attorney can work top-down.

For each finding give the exact location with the question quoted, the problem in one sentence, and a concrete fix, subject to the fix rule.

Close with the point in the claim most exposed as the draft currently frames it, but only if you have the client record. Without it, write "not assessed, no record provided" and do not speculate.
