# Closing Statement (USCIS Asylum Office)

Draft the post-interview attorney closing statement submitted to the USCIS Asylum Office in support of a pending I-589, in the house style of The Law Firm of Nisreen S. Mousa, P.C.

This skill drafts. It does not evaluate whether the claim is viable and it does not build the underlying packet. It takes the record the attorney already has and turns it into the letter that goes to the officer after the interview.

---

## 1. Intake before drafting

Ask for whatever is missing. Do not guess and do not proceed on assumptions about any of these:

**Required**

- Applicant full legal name (as filed) and A number
- Asylum office and the interviewing officer's name (and badge number if used)
- Interview date, if the interview has occurred
- The record: I-589, the applicant's declaration, any witness or family declarations, corroborating exhibits, country conditions materials
- Protected grounds being argued
- Whether there are derivative applicants, and their names

**Ask for, but draft without if unavailable (flag the gap)**

- The attorney's interview notes: what the officer probed, what went badly, what the applicant struggled to explain
- Any adverse facts already on the record: criminal history, prior removals, travel back to the country of feared harm, inconsistencies between the I-589 and testimony, one-year filing delay
- Whether withholding of removal or CAT is being argued in the alternative

If the attorney supplies nothing but a name and says "draft it," stop and ask for the declaration and the I-589. This letter is built entirely from the record; there is nothing to write without it.

**Always ask for the interview notes, every time, even when the packet looks complete.** The interview issues section is the whole point of a closing statement, and it is the one thing that cannot be reconstructed from the packet. The documents show what was filed; only the attorney knows what the officer actually pushed on, where the client stumbled, and what felt like it landed badly. Even a single sentence such as "he hammered the one-year deadline" or "she could not keep the dates straight on the second detention" changes the letter substantially: it decides the format, it decides which issues get their own lettered subsection, and it decides whether the conclusion can call the testimony consistent at all.

Ask in plain terms: what did the officer question, where did the client struggle, and is there anything adverse in the file. If the attorney has no notes, say so in the gap list and draft from the record alone, but do not skip the question.

---

## 2. Choose the format

Two formats appear in the firm's work. Pick one, then state in one line at the top of your response which you picked and why.

### Long numbered format

Roman numeral sections, roughly four to seven pages. Use it when any of these are true:

- The officer raised issues at interview that need to be answered head on
- More than one protected ground is argued
- There are derivative applicants
- There is an adverse fact to address: criminal history, a travel-history question, a filing delay, a bar issue, a gap in corroboration
- The country conditions record is substantial enough to walk through source by source
- The claim has more than one independent theory of harm

### Short narrative format

A flowing letter, no headings, roughly one and a half to two and a half pages. Use it when all of these are true:

- The claim rests on a single clean theory of harm
- Nothing at the interview needs rebutting
- No derivatives, no bars, no adverse facts
- The country conditions point can be made in a sentence or two

When in doubt, go long. A short letter that omits an issue the officer is already thinking about is worse than a long one.

Drop the second sentence, and the reference to family, when neither applies.

## 4. Long format architecture

Use these sections in this order. Merge or drop the ones the case does not support; never invent a section to fill space. Renumber consecutively after any merge or drop.

**I. SUMMARY OF THE ASYLUM CLAIM**
Age, nationality or statelessness, religion or ethnicity where relevant, and the protected grounds, in one sentence. Then who is the principal applicant and who are the derivatives, by name. Then two or three sentences stating the core of what happened and when the applicant fled. This section is the officer's map; it should be readable alone.

**II. EVIDENCE OF PAST PERSECUTION**
Chronological. One incident per paragraph. Each paragraph names the incident, dates it as precisely as the record allows, and closes by naming what it establishes. Then a corroboration paragraph identifying by name each declarant and exhibit that backs the account.

**III. EVIDENCE OF ONGOING PERSECUTION SINCE ARRIVAL IN THE UNITED STATES**
Only if the record shows post-arrival threats or continued targeting. Retitle to `EVIDENCE OF WELL-FOUNDED FEAR OF FUTURE PERSECUTION` when the fear is prospective rather than continuing, with lettered subsections if there is more than one theory of future harm.

**IV. COUNTRY CONDITIONS EVIDENCE**
Open with one sentence stating what the documentary record establishes. Then one paragraph per source. Each paragraph names the source, gives its date, states what it documents, and ties it back to this applicant. Do not list sources without connecting them.

**V. ADDRESSING ISSUES RAISED DURING THE INTERVIEW**
Lettered subsections, one per issue: `A.`, `B.`, `C.`. This is the section that distinguishes a closing statement from a brief, and it is the reason the letter exists. For each issue:

1. Name the issue plainly, in the officer's own terms. Do not soften it.
2. Give the applicant's explanation from the record.
3. Explain why it does not undermine credibility or eligibility.
4. Where possible, turn it around: show why the fact actually corroborates the claim.

Never write around an issue the officer raised. An unanswered question becomes an adverse inference.

**VI. LEGAL STANDARD AND NEXUS TO PROTECTED GROUNDS**
Open: `[Mr./Ms. Surname]'s claim satisfies all elements required for a grant of asylum under INA § 208. [He/She] has established persecution on account of [ground(s)]:`

Then one bolded, labeled paragraph per ground:

- **Religion:** identify the belief or practice and the specific evidence that the persecutor targeted it as such.
- **Political Opinion:** state the opinion, actual or imputed, and explain why the persecutor read the applicant's conduct as political.
- **Membership in a Particular Social Group:** state the group in quotation marks as a formulation, then address immutability or fundamental characteristic, particularity, and social distinction in that society. Where the applicant belongs to more than one group, number them First and Second.

**VII. ABSENCE OF STATE PROTECTION**
Show unable and unwilling separately. Use the country conditions record and any specific instance where the applicant or family sought help and was refused, ignored, or harmed by police. Where the applicant is stateless, retitle to `STATELESSNESS AND LACK OF STATE PROTECTION` and address the absence of any protecting authority first.

**VIII. NO INTERNAL FLIGHT ALTERNATIVE**
Geography, reach of the persecutor, family and community networks, nationwide laws, and known identity. May be merged into VII in shorter letters.

**IX. CONCLUSION**
Three movements, in this order:

1. `For the foregoing reasons, we respectfully request that USCIS grant [full name]'s application for asylum. [His/Her] testimony is credible, detailed, and consistent, and it is corroborated by [list the actual corroboration by category].`
2. A paragraph restating nexus, the failure of state protection, the absence of internal flight, and the consequence of return.
3. A short human paragraph, restrained and specific to this applicant, contrasting what return would mean with what the applicant has found here.

Then:

```
Thank you for your time, compassion, and careful consideration of [Mr./Ms. Surname]'s case.

Respectfully submitted,

Nisreen S Mousa
Attorney at Law
```

Variants in the firm's work: `Thank you for your time and careful consideration of [Mr./Ms. Surname]'s case.` drops "compassion" and reads better on a short, less personal letter. `Regards,` substitutes for `Respectfully submitted,` on the short format. `Nisreen S Mousa` carries no period after the middle initial.

## 5. Short format architecture

No headings. Same letterhead and caption. Paragraphs move in this order:

1. One sentence identifying the applicant, age, nationality or statelessness, and the protected grounds.
2. The core account, two or three paragraphs, chronological, with the attribution phrasing below.
3. Continued or post-departure threats, if any.
4. Why there is no internal relocation and no state protection, in one paragraph.
5. Any status or timing point that needs a sentence, such as maintained lawful status bearing on the one-year deadline.
6. A nexus paragraph naming the grounds expressly and stating that the government is unwilling and unable to protect.
7. The request, the credibility line, the thank you, and the signature block.

Even without headings, every element of eligibility must still appear. Short means compressed, not incomplete.

---

## 6. House style rules

**Attribution.** The firm never asserts a client's facts as independently known. Nearly every factual sentence carries an attribution tag:

- "According to his testimony, ..."
- "According to her statement, ..."
- "Mr. [Name] credibly testified that ..."
- "The record contains substantial evidence establishing that ..."
- "As documented in the country conditions evidence, ..."

Use these consistently. Where a fact comes from a declarant other than the applicant, name that declarant in the sentence.

**Voice.** The firm speaks as "we": we submit, we respectfully request, we wish to clarify, we acknowledge and address. The applicant is always third person and always titled: Mr., Ms., Mrs. Never the first name alone after the first full reference.

**Citations.** The firm's closing statements cite INA § 208 and otherwise argue from the record and the country conditions evidence. They do not string-cite case law. Follow that default. If the attorney supplies case authority, use exactly what was supplied. Never generate a case citation, a reporter number, a docket number, or a quotation from an opinion. If a proposition needs authority you were not given, write the proposition and flag it in the gap list.

**Quotations.** Threats, slurs, and phrases the persecutor used carry the letter. Quote them exactly as they appear in the record, give the translation in parentheses on first use, and never paraphrase them into something milder.

**Exhibits.** Refer to evidence by what it is and who produced it, not by exhibit number, unless the attorney supplies the numbering. "The sworn statement of Pastor [Name] of [Church]" reads correctly; "Exhibit F" invented from nothing does not.

**Punctuation.** Do not use em dashes, en dashes, curly quotation marks, or curly apostrophes anywhere in the output. Use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or a period and a new sentence. Use straight quotes and straight apostrophes only.

**Tone.** Direct, factual, unhurried. The emotional weight comes from the specificity of the facts, not from adjectives. One restrained human paragraph at the end is house style; more than that is not.

**Length discipline.** One idea per paragraph. Every paragraph either advances an element, rebuts an issue, or supplies corroboration. Cut anything that does none of the three.

---

## 7. Hard guardrails

- Never invent a fact, a date, an incident, a name, a threat, an exhibit, a declarant, a country conditions report, or a citation. Everything in the letter traces to the record supplied.
- Never upgrade the record. If the declaration says the applicant was threatened, do not write that he was beaten. If the date is approximate in the record, keep it approximate in the letter.
- Where the record is thin on something the letter needs, write the sentence the record supports and put the gap in the gap list. Do not fill it.
- Do not characterize testimony as consistent, detailed, or credible in a way that contradicts what the attorney told you about the interview. If testimony was shaky on a point, the letter addresses that point in Section V rather than claiming everything went smoothly.
- Country conditions sources must be ones the attorney actually submitted or supplied. Do not add a report because it is well known and probably says the relevant thing.

---

## 8. Deliverables

Return, in this order:

1. **Format choice**, one line, with the reason.
2. **The closing statement**, complete and signature ready. Produce it as a .docx on the firm letterhead when a document is wanted; otherwise inline.
3. **Gap list.** Every place the record did not support what the letter needed, and what would close it. Bracketed placeholders left in the draft are listed here.
4. **Issue coverage check.** A short table: each issue the officer raised at interview, and where in the letter it is answered. Any issue with no row is a blocker.
5. **Element coverage check.** Past persecution or well founded fear, nexus to each ground argued, state protection, internal relocation, one year deadline if applicable, bars if applicable. Mark each as covered, thin, or absent.

---

## 9. Self review before delivering

Run this against your own draft and fix what fails:

- Caption complete: correct office, correct officer name, applicant name in all capitals, A number correctly spaced, correct date.
- Every issue the attorney flagged from the interview has its own lettered subsection or its own paragraph.
- Every protected ground argued in Section I is also argued in the nexus section, and no ground appears in one but not the other.
- Every PSG is stated as a formulation in quotation marks and tested on immutability, particularity, and social distinction.
- State protection addresses both unable and unwilling.
- Internal relocation is closed, not merely asserted to be unavailable.
- Every derivative applicant is named.
- Every country conditions source is named, dated, and tied to this applicant.
- Every factual sentence about the applicant's experience carries an attribution tag.
- No case citation appears that the attorney did not supply.
- No em dashes, en dashes, or curly quotes anywhere.
- The conclusion lists corroboration that actually exists in the record.
- No sentence claims more than the record supports.

State plainly what this draft is: attorney work product prepared for review and signature by the responsible attorney, not a filing ready document until the attorney has verified every fact, name, date, and source against the file.
