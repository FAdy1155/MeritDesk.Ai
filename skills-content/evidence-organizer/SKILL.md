# Evidence Organizer

Turn a pile of documents into a filing-ready, element-mapped record, and surface what's still missing — as **attorney work product** the attorney reviews before filing.

## Before anything

Read `PRACTICE_PROFILE.md` for **house style** (tabbing/exhibit convention, index format, pagination), **evidence standards** (accepted country-conditions sources and recency, the firm's expected corroboration set), and the **filing source of truth** (the current Immigration Court Practice Manual for EOIR, or Asylum Office practice for affirmative cases). Deadlines, cover-page, and service rules must be verified against the current manual and the specific court's local operating procedures — do not assert a fixed number of days from memory.

Confirm the venue: **EOIR (defensive)** filings follow the ICPM; **USCIS Asylum Office (affirmative)** submissions follow Asylum Office practice. The structure below is the same; the cover sheet, filing mechanics, and deadlines differ.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory** every document provided. For each: what it is, source, date, language, and whether it's an original, copy, or needs authentication.

2. **Map each document to the element it supports.** Use the same elements as `claim-evaluation` (protected ground, PSG prongs, nexus, persecution/harm, state action, internal relocation, credibility, timeliness). An exhibit that supports no element is either background or clutter — flag it.

3. **Build the exhibit index** in the firm's house-style tabbing (e.g., Tab A, Tab B…). Produce an **Index of Documents** / table of contents keyed to tabs, with a one-line description and the element each supports.

4. **Country-conditions check.** Confirm sources are within the firm's tier and recency rules. Core reports (DOS Country Reports, UNHCR, HRW, Amnesty) should anchor the packet; note anything stale or thin, and flag where an **expert declaration** is expected per the profile.

5. **Corroboration gap list.** Compare the record to the firm's expected corroboration set. For each gap, state whether it appears **reasonably available** (obtain it) or should be documented as **not reasonably available** on the record — this matters under the REAL ID Act corroboration rule.

6. **Translation & certification.** Any foreign-language document needs a full certified English translation with a translator's certificate of competence (8 CFR 1003.33 / ICPM). List every document still needing translation or certification.

7. **Filing checklist.** Assemble the mechanical requirements: cover page, index of documents, pagination per house style, **proof of service on the DHS/ICE Office of the Principal Legal Advisor**, and the filing deadline for the venue — flagged for the attorney to confirm against the ICPM and the court's LOP. For ECAS e-filing, note the electronic submission steps if that integration is enabled.

## Output format

Under the work-product header, produce:

1. **Exhibit index** — Tab | Document | Date | Element(s) supported | Language / translation status.
2. **Element-to-evidence matrix** — each element with the exhibits that support it, so thin elements are visible at a glance.
3. **Corroboration gaps** — missing item | reasonably available? | action.
4. **Country-conditions status** — sources present, currency, and any recommended additions or expert declaration.
5. **Translation/certification to-do.**
6. **Filing checklist** — with deadlines and service requirements flagged "confirm against ICPM / court LOP."

## Guardrails

- Organizing the record is not filing it and not advising the client. The attorney reviews and files.
- Do not assert filing deadlines, page limits, or service rules as fixed facts from memory — cite them to "the current ICPM / court LOP" for attorney confirmation, because they vary by court and change.
- Do not fabricate the contents of a document you haven't been given; if a described exhibit isn't provided, list it as expected-but-not-received.
- Never alter, backdate, or characterize a document as saying something it doesn't. Integrity of the record is the whole point.
- Respect the heightened confidentiality of asylum records.
