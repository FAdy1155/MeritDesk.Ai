# I-90 Packet Build (Green Card Renewal and Replacement)

Take what the attorney has for an I-90 client (intake notes, a copy of the card, identity documents, prior notices) and assemble a filing-ready packet: the reason-for-filing determination, the Form I-90 answer table and filled PDF, the G-28, the cover letter, the evidence index, the fee and filing checklist, and the client instruction sheet. Then run the QA gate over the finished product.

This is attorney work product and a drafting aid. It is not a filing decision and not advice to the client. The supervising attorney resolves every flagged item before anything is filed.

## Scope

**In scope.** Lawful permanent residents holding a ten year card who need it renewed or replaced:

- Card has expired, or will expire within six months
- Card was lost
- Card was stolen
- Card was destroyed
- Card is mutilated
- Card was issued but never received

**Out of scope. Say so and stop.** Legal name or biographic change; incorrect data from a DHS error; incorrect data from the applicant's own error; the age 14 biometrics replacement; commuter status changes; automatic conversion to permanent resident status; and legacy AR-3, AR-103, or I-151 cards. Each has its own evidence and fee treatment. Also out of scope: any request to remove conditions on residence, which is Form I-751 or Form I-829, never Form I-90.

---

## SECTION 0. HARD RULES

These never bend.

1. **Never invent a fact.** Building a strong packet means better articulation, completeness, and organization of facts the client actually gave, plus identifying what is still to gather. Where there is a gap, insert a bracketed flag: `[CLARIFY WITH CLIENT: approximate date the wallet was taken?]` or `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: she has lived at the Bellaire address since March 2023]`. Never suggest the client add a detail because it would help.
2. **Never invent evidence.** Do not write a cover letter enclosure line for a document that does not exist. Wanted but absent documents go on the evidence request list, not the enclosure list.
3. **Never invent a citation.** Use only the authority in Section 3 or authority retrieved and read in this session. Anything else gets `[VERIFY CITE]`.
4. **Never state a form edition, fee, filing address, or extension period from memory.** See Section 2.
5. **Plain ASCII output.** No em dashes, no smart quotes, no special characters, in any deliverable.
6. **Screen before you build.** The threshold gates in Section 1 come before any drafting. An I-90 is a low complexity form that puts the client in front of USCIS for biometrics and a background check. If the client has a removal, abandonment, or criminal issue, the form is the easy part and the screening is the whole job.

## SECTION 1. INTAKE, ROUTING GATES, AND MATERIAL INVENTORY

Do this before drafting anything. Post it at the top of the output.

**Inventory.** List exactly what was provided, by document. List what a complete packet for this posture needs and was NOT provided. Never assume the contents of a document you were not given.

**Client posture.** State each of these or flag it unknown:

- Full legal name as it appears on the card, and every other name ever used
- A-number, USCIS online account number if any, and date of birth
- Class of admission code and date of admission or adjustment, exactly as printed on the card
- Card issue date, expiration date, and whether the card is a ten year card or a two year card
- Current mailing address and physical address, how long at each, and whether mail is reliably received there
- Current location: inside the United States or abroad
- Full travel history since becoming an LPR, with every absence of six months or more
- Criminal history, arrests, and citations of any kind
- Removal, deportation, exclusion, or rescission history, past or pending
- Any prior I-90, N-400, I-751, or I-829, and its outcome
- Whether an N-400 is filed, imminent, or contemplated
- Whether the client needs proof of status urgently, for travel or for employment
- Ability to pay the fee, for the fee waiver question

**Six routing gates. Resolve each explicitly and say what turns on it.**

1. **Two year card, conditional resident.** A conditional permanent resident may not use Form I-90 to remove conditions, and is **not eligible to file an I-90 for any reason during the ninety day period before the second anniversary of obtaining CPR status**. Route to Form I-751 (marriage based) or Form I-829 (investor). Stop the build and say so.
2. **Pending or imminent naturalization.** If an N-400 is filed or about to be filed, the N-400 receipt notice itself extends the validity of the expired card for a stated period, and a separate I-90 may be unnecessary. Verify the current extension period at run time. Build the I-90 anyway only where the client has a concrete need the N-400 receipt will not meet, and say what that need is.
3. **Client is outside the United States.** An I-90 is generally not the vehicle for a resident abroad without a card. A returning resident who lost the card abroad ordinarily needs a boarding foil through the consulate (Form I-131A) to travel, and files the I-90 after returning. Verify the current procedure and route accordingly. Do not build a mail-from-abroad packet by default.
4. **Abandonment exposure.** Any absence approaching or exceeding one year without a reentry permit, a pattern of long absences, a home, job, or tax filing posture abroad, or a nonresident tax return, is an abandonment issue. Filing an I-90 invites USCIS to look. Flag it, do not paper over it, and escalate to the supervising attorney before filing.
5. **Removal posture and criminal history.** Any arrest, charge, conviction, prior order, pending proceeding, or prior rescission means the packet does not go out until the attorney has run a removability and inadmissibility analysis. Part 3 of the form asks about proceedings directly and the answer must be truthful. Escalate.
6. **Identity and status proof.** If the client cannot produce the card or any secondary proof of status, say what secondary proof exists or must be obtained (passport with I-551 or ADIT stamp, immigrant visa page, I-797 approval notice, prior copies of the card, USCIS records request). A packet with no proof of status is not ready.

If a gate cannot be resolved from the material, put it in a **client and attorney question list** at the top of the output and build around the gap rather than guessing.

## SECTION 2. RUN-TIME VERIFICATION

Before building the form, confirm against **uscis.gov** and state what you verified and on what date:

- Current **edition date** of Form I-90 and of Form G-28, and whether older editions are still accepted. A superseded edition gets rejected.
- Current **filing fee**, separately for **online filing** and **paper filing**, and whether any biometric services fee is charged separately.
- Which **reasons for filing are fee exempt**, and the exact conditions.
- Whether **Form I-912** fee waivers are available for the I-90 and what the current income and hardship criteria are.
- Current **direct filing address** for a paper I-90. The USPS address and the courier or express address are different. Put the address matching the office's chosen mailing method in the cover letter block.
- Whether **online filing is available for this specific reason for filing**, and whether the attorney can represent the client through a USCIS online account.
- The **current automatic extension period** the I-90 receipt notice gives an expired card, and the exact language the notice uses. As last confirmed this was extended to thirty six months, but state it only after verifying.
- Current **processing times** for the I-90 at the adjudicating office, for the client instruction sheet.
- Whether the **ADIT or I-551 stamp** appointment process for interim proof of status has changed.

If a fetch fails, say so and mark the item `[ATTORNEY: VERIFY]`. Do not substitute memory.

**Reference values, last confirmed and always to be re-verified:** form edition 01/20/25; online filing fee $415; paper filing fee $465; no separate biometric services fee; fee exempt where USCIS made an error on the card or where USCIS mailed the card and it was returned undeliverable through no fault of the applicant; receipt notice extension of thirty six months. Treat every one of these as `[VERIFY]` until confirmed in session.

## SECTION 3. THE STANDARD TO BUILD TO

**Anchor authority.** INA 264(d) and (e) (entitlement to evidence of status, and the requirement that an LPR be issued and carry a card); 8 CFR 264.5 (replacement of the Permanent Resident Card: grounds, application, biometrics, decision); INA 216 and 216A (conditional residence, which is why a CPR cannot use this form to remove conditions); 8 CFR 103.2(b) (filing and evidence generally); 8 CFR 103.5 (motions, which are the only recourse from a denial); **USCIS Policy Manual Volume 11, Part B, Chapter 2** (Replacement of Permanent Resident Card). Anything beyond these gets `[VERIFY CITE]`.

**What the adjudication actually tests.** This is not a benefits case. USCIS is deciding three things: that the applicant is who they say they are, that the applicant holds the status claimed, and that a replacement ground exists. Build to those three and nothing else. Do not pad the packet with material that invites unrelated inquiry.

**Post-filing mechanics to build the client instruction sheet around.**

- The applicant must appear at an Application Support Center for photograph, signature, and fingerprints. **Failure to appear within the stated window can be treated as abandonment and the application denied.** Calendar it.
- The receipt notice, presented **together with the expired card**, extends the validity of the card for the period stated on the notice. Tell the client to keep them together and never separate them.
- There is **no appeal** from an I-90 denial. The only recourse is a motion to reopen or reconsider under 8 CFR 103.5. That makes a clean, complete filing worth more here than in forms with an appeal path.
- USCIS mails the card to the address on file and cards are generally not forwarded. Confirm the address is current, deliverable, and correctly formatted, and calendar Form AR-11 or the online address change for any move while the case is pending. Address failure is the single most common cause of a never-received card and a second I-90.

## SECTION 4. THE REASON FOR FILING, AND THE EVIDENCE THAT GOES WITH IT

Fix the Part 2 reason first. It drives the fee, the evidence, and the filing channel. Only one reason is checked. If the facts fit more than one, say so and explain the choice.

| Reason | When it is correct | Evidence to enclose |
|---|---|---|
| Card expired or expires within six months | Ten year card at or near expiration, still in the client's possession | Clear copy of the card, front and back |
| Card lost | Client no longer has the card and does not know where it is | Any copy of the card that exists; if none, secondary proof of status (passport with immigrant visa or I-551 or ADIT stamp, I-797 approval notice, prior photocopy); government issued photo ID; a short factual statement of when and how it was last seen |
| Card stolen | Card taken in a theft, burglary, or robbery | Same as lost, plus the police report or report number if one was made. A report is not required. Never tell a client to file one that does not reflect what happened |
| Card destroyed | Card destroyed in a fire, flood, laundering, or similar | Same as lost, plus any documentation of the event that already exists |
| Card mutilated | Card damaged but still in the client's possession | The **original mutilated card must be submitted**, not a copy. Copy it for the file before it goes |
| Card issued but never received | USCIS approved and produced a card the client never got | Copy of the I-797 approval notice or other evidence the card was issued; government issued photo ID; the address of record at the time and the client's actual address then; evidence the card was returned to USCIS as undeliverable, if that is what happened |

**Never received, fee treatment.** The fee exemption depends on the card having gone undeliverable through no fault of the applicant. Where the address on file was wrong because of something the client did or failed to do, the full fee applies. Verify the current rule at run time and state the basis for whichever position the packet takes.

**Every reason also needs:** proof of identity and proof of LPR status. Where the card itself is unavailable, the packet must carry that weight through secondary evidence, and the cover letter must say so explicitly rather than leaving USCIS to notice the gap.

## SECTION 5. FORM I-90, PART BY PART

Produce a **question-by-question answer table** with columns: Part and item number | Question | Answer | Source of the answer | Flag. Then fill the fillable PDF from the table so the two always agree.

**Part 1, information about the applicant.** Name exactly as on the card, plus every other name ever used, including maiden names and names on prior filings. Mailing address and physical address, with an in-care-of line where the client receives mail through someone else. Date of birth, country of birth, country of citizenship. A-number, digits only, matching the card. USCIS online account number if one exists. Class of admission code and date of admission or adjustment, **transcribed exactly from the card** and never reconstructed from memory. Parents' names. No item left blank; use None or N/A.

**Part 2, application type and reason.** One reason, per Section 4.

**Part 3, processing information.** Location where status was granted, destination consulate or port where applicable, and the **proceedings question**. Any yes here stops the build and goes to the attorney. Do not soften, omit, or explain away a yes.

**Part 4, accommodations.** Complete if the client needs a disability accommodation at the ASC appointment.

**Part 5, statement, contact, certification, signature.** Correct box for whether the client read the form in English or had it read in a language they understand. Daytime phone, mobile, and email. Wet ink or valid electronic signature, dated. An unsigned form is rejected, not RFE'd.

**Part 6, interpreter.** Complete in full whenever the form was read to the client in another language. Name, business, address, phone, language, signature, date.

**Part 7, preparer.** Complete whenever anyone other than the client prepared the form, including the office. Check the attorney box where a G-28 is filed and confirm the preparer information matches the G-28.

**Part 8, additional information.** Use it. Continuation entries carry the page, part, and item number. This is where the lost-card narrative, the never-received explanation, and any name-variance explanation belong.

**Cross-check before finalizing.** A-number, name spelling, date of birth, class of admission, and date of admission must be identical across the card, the form, the G-28, the cover letter, and every enclosure. Any variance is listed, not silently reconciled.

## SECTION 6. ONLINE VERSUS PAPER

Default to **online filing** through the client's USCIS online account with the office linked as representative. It costs less, produces an immediate receipt, and gives the office live case status and the extension notice faster.

**File on paper when any of these is true:**

- A fee waiver on Form I-912 is being requested
- An original document must be physically submitted, which for this scope means a **mutilated card**
- Online filing is not available for the reason selected, verified at run time
- The client cannot maintain a USCIS online account or cannot sign electronically
- The office has a documented reason to prefer a mailed record

**If online.** Confirm the account is the client's own, not the office's; confirm representative linkage and the G-28 upload; convert every enclosure to a clean, legible, correctly oriented scan within the size and format limits; pay by the accepted method; download and save the receipt notice the same day.

**If paper.** Confirm the current direct filing address and match it to the mailing method. Assemble in order: payment or fee waiver, G-28, cover letter, Form I-90, then tabbed enclosures. Two hole punch at the top if the office's practice is to do so. Sign in black ink. Ship trackable and calendar the delivery.

---

## SECTION 7. DELIVERABLES

Produce all of these, in this order, in plain ASCII, under a header reading **Attorney Work Product, Privileged and Confidential**, with the preparer and date:

1. **Case snapshot and routing determination.** Client, A-number, card type and dates, reason for filing, filing channel, fee and basis, and the result of each of the six gates in Section 1.
2. **Client and attorney question list.** Everything that must be answered before filing.
3. **Run-time verification log.** Each item from Section 2, what was confirmed, the source, and the date. Unverified items marked `[ATTORNEY: VERIFY]`.
4. **Form I-90 answer table.** Question by question, per Section 5, with sources and flags.
5. **Filled Form I-90 PDF.** Populated from the answer table, current edition, signature and date fields left for wet signature where paper.
6. **Form G-28**, completed for the office and the client.
7. **Cover letter.** Client and A-number, the reason for filing and the ground in one sentence, the fee or fee exemption basis with its reason, and a numbered enclosure list that matches the tabs exactly. Short. This filing does not want argument.
8. **Evidence index.** Tab number, document, what it proves, original or copy, and translation status. Certified English translation for any foreign language document, with the translator certification attached.
9. **Evidence request list.** Everything still to be gathered, who gets it, and why the packet is weaker without it. Never merged into the enclosure list.
10. **Fee and filing checklist.** Amount, payment method, payee, channel, address matched to mailing method, edition date confirmed, every signature confirmed, tracking, and the file copy.
11. **Client instruction sheet.** In plain language: what was filed and why; keep the receipt notice with the expired card at all times and never separate them; the ASC biometrics appointment is mandatory and missing it can end the case; report any address change to USCIS immediately and how; how to request an ADIT stamp if urgent proof of status is needed before the card arrives; do not travel outside the United States without speaking to the office first; expected processing time.
12. **Calendar items.** ASC appointment window, follow up date if no receipt within the expected window, card delivery follow up, address change deadlines, and the extension expiration date on the receipt notice.

## SECTION 8. QA GATE

When the packet is assembled, **run `i90-packet-review` over your own output** and append its report. Resolve or flag every blocking item it returns. Do not present the packet as filing ready while a blocking item is open.

## Guardrails

- Work product for the attorney. Not a filing decision, not client advice, and not a determination that the client holds or retains lawful permanent resident status.
- Never invent facts, evidence, or citations. Strengthen only the articulation and organization of what the client actually has.
- Never state a fee, edition, address, or extension period from memory.
- Never build past a routing gate. Conditional residence, abandonment exposure, criminal history, and removal posture go to the supervising attorney before anything is filed.
- Never advise a client to obtain a police report, or any other document, that would not reflect what actually happened.
- Respect the confidentiality of the client's immigration records.
