# Worked examples

Before/after for each category. The lesson threaded through all of them: fix the
certain, flag the uncertain.

## 1. Spelling and typos

Fix (certain):
- "The applicant recieved the notice." -> "The applicant received the notice."
- "She was detianed at the border." -> "She was detained at the border."

Flag (uncertain): a real word that might be intended.
- "He was their at the hearing." Likely "there," but confirm it is not a
  deliberate construction. Low risk, so fixing is usually fine; note it.

## 2. Diacritics and accents on names

Fix (certain): the accented form appears elsewhere in the same document.
- Heading reads "Declaration of Jose Nunez"; body says "Jose stated." Align the
  body to the accented "Jose" used in the heading.

Flag (uncertain): you have never seen the name accented in this document.
- "Munoz" appears throughout with no accent and no signature to confirm. Do not
  add an accent on a guess; flag and ask whether it should be "Munoz."

## 3. Consistent spelling of names and terms

Fix (certain): one clear majority spelling.
- "Guatemala" appears 11 times and "Guatamala" once. Standardize the one to the
  eleven.

Flag (uncertain): two plausible spellings, could be two people.
- "Nisreen Mousa" in the caption, "Nesreen Mousa" in paragraph 6. Flag it: this
  could be a typo, or a different person. Let the author decide.

## 4. Singular/plural (number) agreement

Fix (certain):
- "The documents was submitted on time." -> "The documents were submitted on
  time."
- "These evidence supports her claim." -> "This evidence supports her claim."

Flag (uncertain): the subject is a collective or the intent is unclear.
- "The board have decided." British usage treats collectives as plural; if the
  document is otherwise US style, note it rather than forcing a change.

## 5. Pronoun consistency and agreement

Fix (certain): a clear antecedent number mismatch with no gender question.
- "Each applicant must file their own form" is widely accepted; leave it. But
  "The witness gave their testimony; he then left" where the witness is
  consistently "he" - align "their" to "his."

Flag (uncertain): the referent's gender or identity is unclear.
- A declarant is "he" in paragraphs 1 to 4 and "she" in paragraph 5. Do not pick
  one silently; this can read as a factual inconsistency in a declaration. Flag
  it prominently and ask which is correct.
