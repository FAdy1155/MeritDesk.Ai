# EOIR Motions Reference

The legal-argument-research Skill reads this file when the question is a motion. It was missing from the original Skill. Version 0.2.0 of eoir-court-practice created it so the Skill can see recent motions law, and the eoir-weekly-update Skill adds to it every week.

## Status of this file

- The motion by motion standards section (governing standard, anchor authority, deadlines and limits, pitfalls) has not been written yet. Until it is, take the standard and anchor authority from retrieved, verified research, and use the element structure in the motion-elements.md file of the eoir-motion-self-review Skill in this plugin as a cross check.
- Deadlines and filing mechanics for motions are in the icpm-mechanics.md file of eoir-motion-self-review and the icpm-checklist.md and bia-checklist.md files of eoir-filing-compliance.
- Everything under Recent updates below was added from official sources and must be verified against its link before it is relied on.

## Volatile areas

The Skill's currency rule names administrative closure and NTA defect law as areas that have reversed direction repeatedly. Treat in absentia notice law and sua sponte reopening the same way: see the 2026 entries below. For any volatile area, verify the current state of the law with web tools and report the date through which it was confirmed.

### Matter of Diji, 30 I&N Dec. 1 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4240
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1460146/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: (1) When mailing a notice to appear by regular mail, the Department of Homeland Security can rely on the most recent address the alien has provided. (2) For purposes of an in absentia removal proceeding, written notice of the proceedings is sufficient if mailed to the most recent address the alien provided, which includes an address the alien provided before the issuance of the notice to appear. *Matter of G-Y-R-*, 23 I&N Dec. 181 (BIA 2001) (en banc), overruled.
- Check above: Volatile area, in absentia notice. Overrules Matter of G-Y-R-, 23 I&N Dec. 181.

### Matter of A-V-O-, 30 I&N Dec. 44 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4245
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1461651/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: An alien who has been convicted of an aggravated felony is inadmissible under section 212(a)(8)(A) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(8)(A) (2024), as an alien who is ineligible to citizenship. *Matter of Kanga*, 22 I&N Dec. 1206 (BIA 2000), overruled.
- Check above: Adjustment based motions: an aggravated felony conviction is an inadmissibility bar under INA 212(a)(8)(A). Overrules Matter of Kanga, 22 I&N Dec. 1206.

### Matter of Mendez Maldonado, 30 I&N Dec. 62 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4247
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1462126/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: (1) The respondent's admission to possessing and using cocaine makes him inadmissible under section 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(II) of the Immigration and Nationality Act ("INA"), 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(2)(A)(i)(II) (2024), and hence ineligible for adjustment of status. (2) Section 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(II) of the INA, 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(2)(A)(i)(II), does not require that the alien be informed of the precise statutory definition with the essential elements of the offense in question prior to making an admission. *Matter of K-*, 7 I&N Dec. 594 (BIA 1957), and *Matter of J-*, 2 I&N Dec. 285 (BIA 1945), overruled.
- Check above: Adjustment based motions: an admission to drug possession and use can bar adjustment under INA 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(II). Overrules Matter of K- and Matter of J-.

### Matter of K-K-U-J-, 30 I&N Dec. 81 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4249
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1462356/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: The Immigration Judge erred in finding exceptional circumstances warranting sua sponte reopening and rescission of an in absentia removal order where the record did not support the respondent's claim that an individual misled him into believing he did not need to appear in Immigration Court.
- Check above: Volatile area, sua sponte reopening and rescission of in absentia orders.

### Matter of M-K-, 30 I&N Dec. 84 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4250
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1462911/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: Reopening is unwarranted because the respondent's claim of a lack of due process is speculative, based on a series of false assumptions and fallacious reasoning, and wholly unsupported by the actual evidence in the record.
- Check above: Motions to reopen on due process grounds: speculative claims unsupported by the record fail.

### Matter of C-M-H-H-, 30 I&N Dec. 124 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4256
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1463836/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: (1) The Board does not retain jurisdiction after remanding the record to the Immigration Court. *Matter of Patel*, 16 I&N Dec. 600, 601 (BIA 1978), clarified. (2) The Immigration Judge's issuance of a new decision on remand without holding another hearing did not violate the Board's remand order nor deprive the respondent of due process.
- Check above: Motions after a remand: the Board does not retain jurisdiction after remanding the record. Clarifies Matter of Patel, 16 I&N Dec. 600.
