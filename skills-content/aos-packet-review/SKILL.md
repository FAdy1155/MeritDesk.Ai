# AOS Packet Review — Marriage to a U.S. Citizen

Do the last careful read of an assembled marriage-based adjustment-of-status packet and hand the reviewer a report they can act on: what must be fixed before filing, what would make it stronger, whether the beneficiary is actually eligible to adjust, and where the marriage evidence is thin. This is **work product and a QA gate — not a filing decision, not legal advice to the client, and not a determination that the marriage is bona fide.**

## Before anything

Confirm the basics of the case: petitioner (U.S. citizen spouse) and beneficiary names, whether the beneficiary is **in the U.S. and eligible to adjust** (this skill is for concurrent or standalone I-485 filings, not consular processing), the **date of the marriage** and whether it will be under two years at approval (conditional residence / later I-751), the **manner of last entry** (inspected & admitted or paroled vs. entered without inspection), and any prior immigration or criminal history. If you cannot confirm a fact from the packet, list it as *not provided* rather than assuming it.

Confirm what is actually in the packet before reviewing it. A typical concurrent marriage-based AOS packet contains:

- **Form I-130**, Petition for Alien Relative (filed by the U.S. citizen spouse) and **Form I-130A**, Supplemental Information for Spouse Beneficiary
- **Form I-485**, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status (beneficiary)
- **Form I-864**, Affidavit of Support (petitioner as sponsor) and **Form I-864A** if a household member or joint sponsor is used
- **Form I-693**, Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record (sealed by the civil surgeon)
- **Form I-765** (employment authorization) and/or **Form I-131** (advance parole), if filed concurrently
- **Proof of the petitioner's U.S. citizenship** (birth certificate, U.S. passport, naturalization/citizenship certificate)
- **Marriage certificate**, plus **certified proof of termination of all prior marriages** for both spouses (divorce decrees, annulments, death certificates)
- **Beneficiary's proof of lawful entry / inspection** (passport, visa, I-94, admission or parole stamp)
- **Beneficiary's birth certificate** with certified English translation
- **Bona fide marriage evidence** (joint financial, cohabitation, insurance, children, photos, affidavits, correspondence)
- **Passport-style photos**, filing fees / fee waiver request, and any G-1145 / G-28

If a component is missing, list it as *not provided* rather than assuming its contents. Note that the **I-693 medical is sealed** — do not ask for it to be opened; only confirm it is present, current, and from a civil surgeon.

## The two integrity rules

1. **Never invent facts to make an answer or the marriage look "stronger."** Strengthening means better *articulation, completeness, organization, and coverage of facts and documents the couple actually has* — plus identifying the specific items to go **gather or confirm** (flagged `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: …]` or `[OBTAIN: …]`). Never suggest the couple add a document, a shared account, or a detail because it would help the case if it does not reflect reality. Marriage fraud is a federal crime under INA § 275(c); manufacturing evidence of a marriage destroys the case and exposes everyone involved.
2. **Never fabricate citations.** Offer only well-established anchor authority you are confident of (statutes, regulations, and the USCIS Policy Manual). For anything you are unsure of, use a placeholder — `[CITE: USCIS Policy Manual / controlling authority — attorney to confirm]` — and flag every citation for attorney verification.

## What to review

### A. Form I-130 / I-130A — the qualifying relationship
- **Petitioner eligibility and status** — U.S. citizen; citizenship evidence attached and matches the form.
- **Beneficiary information** — biographic data consistent across I-130, I-130A, and I-485; all A-numbers, aliases, and prior filings disclosed.
- **Prior marriages** — every prior marriage of *both* spouses listed and documented as terminated before the current marriage date. A gap here is a common, fatal defect.
- **Prior I-130 petitions** — any earlier petition filed by or for either party disclosed (a prior petition, especially a prior marriage-based one, can trigger a fraud inquiry under INA § 204(c)).
- **I-130A** — beneficiary's employment and address history complete for the required period; signature present (or explained if beneficiary cannot sign).
- No blanks — unanswered items should read "None" or "N/A," not empty.

### B. Form I-485 — question by question
- **Eligibility category** — the correct immediate-relative basis (spouse of U.S. citizen) is selected and matches the I-130.
- **Manner of entry / immigration history** — last entry, I-94, status history, and any periods of unlawful presence or unauthorized employment stated accurately and consistently with the I-130A and evidence.
- **Part 8 — the inadmissibility and eligibility questions** (criminal, security, public charge, immigration violations, prior removals, fraud/misrepresentation, membership, etc.). Read every "Yes/No" for accuracy and for **agreement with the rest of the packet**. A "No" that the record contradicts is a misrepresentation risk under INA § 212(a)(6)(C).
- **Address, biographic, and background sections** — complete; no blanks.
- **Signature and date** — beneficiary's signature present and dated; interpreter and preparer sections completed if applicable.
- Flag every blank, every internal inconsistency, and every answer that conflicts with the I-130, I-130A, or supporting evidence.

### C. Adjustment eligibility & bars — INA § 245
Step back and test whether the beneficiary can actually adjust:
- **§ 245(a) threshold** — was the beneficiary **inspected and admitted or paroled**? If they **entered without inspection (EWI)**, flag that adjustment generally is unavailable unless grandfathered under **§ 245(i)** — confirm and escalate.
- **Immediate-relative status** — spouse of a U.S. citizen is an immediate relative, so a visa number is always available and the **§ 245(c)** bars for unlawful presence, unauthorized employment, and failure to maintain status generally **do not apply**. Confirm the beneficiary truly qualifies as an immediate relative (valid, subsisting marriage to a U.S. citizen) and note that the § 245(c) exemption does **not** cure an EWI entry.
- **§ 204(c) marriage-fraud bar** — any indication of a prior sham marriage or a prior finding/attempt of marriage fraud is a hard bar; escalate immediately.
- **Other bars** — J-1/J-2 two-year foreign residence (§ 212(e)), conditional-basis issues, prior removal orders, or visa-waiver-entry complications. Flag any that appear.

### D. Inadmissibility — INA § 212(a)
Check whether any ground of inadmissibility is triggered and whether a waiver is set up if needed:
- **Health-related** (§ 212(a)(1)) — I-693 vaccinations and any Class A/B conditions.
- **Criminal** (§ 212(a)(2)) — any arrest/charge/conviction disclosed and analyzed; certified dispositions obtained; CIMT / controlled-substance issues escalated.
- **Public charge** (§ 212(a)(4)) — supported by an adequate I-864 (see below); the totality-of-circumstances factors on the I-485 are consistent.
- **Fraud / misrepresentation** (§ 212(a)(6)(C)) and **unlawful presence** (§ 212(a)(9)(B)/(C)) — note that unlawful presence bars are triggered by **departure**, so an in-country adjustor generally does not trip them, but flag any prior departures, prior removals, or reentries after unlawful presence.
- Flag any ground that appears triggered and whether the correct **waiver** (e.g., I-601) is contemplated.

### E. Form I-864 Affidavit of Support — the money
- **Sponsor obligation** — petitioner is the required sponsor; form signed and dated.
- **Household size** — counted correctly (sponsor + beneficiary + dependents + anyone previously sponsored).
- **Income test** — sponsor's income meets **125% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines** for that household size (100% for qualifying active-duty military sponsoring a spouse). Flag if it does not.
- **Evidence** — most recent federal tax return (or transcript), W-2s/1099s, and current income proof (pay stubs / employer letter) attached and internally consistent.
- **Joint sponsor / household member** — if income is short, confirm an **I-864 (joint sponsor)** or **I-864A (household member)** is included, complete, signed, and independently meets the threshold.
- Flag every math inconsistency, missing tax year, and gap between stated income and attached proof.

### F. Bona fide marriage evidence
Assess whether the evidence, as assembled, actually demonstrates a genuine marital relationship — the heart of the case:
- **Coverage across categories** — commingled finances (joint accounts, joint tax returns/filing status, shared credit), cohabitation (lease/mortgage/deed in both names, matching addresses, utilities), interdependence (health/life/auto insurance naming each other, beneficiary designations), children of the marriage, and social proof (photos across time, travel, correspondence, affidavits from people who know the couple).
- **Depth and timeline** — evidence spans the length of the relationship, not just a burst around the filing date. Note thin categories.
- **Red-flag sensitivity** — where the record is thin, describe **what additional real documentation to gather** (`[OBTAIN: …]`) or **what facts to confirm with the couple** (`[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: …]`). Never suggest creating a shared account, backdating, or manufacturing proof. If genuine indicators of concern appear (no cohabitation, large unexplained gaps, contradictory histories), surface them for the attorney rather than papering over them.

### G. Cross-document consistency
Reconcile the I-130 ↔ I-130A ↔ I-485 ↔ I-864 ↔ supporting evidence ↔ any prior filing (prior I-130s, prior visa applications, I-94 record). List every discrepancy — names, dates of birth, marriage/divorce dates, addresses, entry dates, employment, household size, income. Inconsistency across the packet is exactly what an adjudicator uses at the interview to question credibility and bona fides. Do not silently reconcile discrepancies — surface each one, with where it appears, for the attorney and client to resolve.

### H. Authorities to review
Give the reviewer a short, targeted reading list — anchors plus placeholders for anything to verify. Frame these as *authorities to review and confirm*, never as settled conclusions, and never fabricate one. Reliable anchors: INA §§ 201(b) (immediate relatives), 204(a)/(c) (petition & marriage-fraud bar), 212(a) (inadmissibility), 245(a)/(c)/(i) (adjustment & bars); 8 CFR 204, 245; the **USCIS Policy Manual** (Vol. 7 — Adjustment of Status; Vol. 8 — Admissibility); and the current **HHS Federal Poverty Guidelines** and Form I-864P for the income test.

## Report format

ALWAYS produce, under a work-product header (reviewer, date, "Attorney Work Product — Privileged & Confidential"):

1. **Packet snapshot** — petitioner, beneficiary, marriage date (and whether conditional at approval), manner of entry, filing type (concurrent vs. standalone), and the exact list of documents reviewed (and any *not provided*).
2. **Blocking issues** — things that must be fixed before filing: missing signature, blank required questions, unlisted/undocumented prior marriage, I-864 income below threshold with no joint sponsor, missing or expired I-693, missing proof of petitioner's citizenship, unresolved contradiction, or an unaddressed adjustment bar (EWI without 245(i), possible 204(c)). Nothing files until these clear.
3. **Form findings** — form-by-form (I-130/I-130A, I-485, I-864/I-864A): issue | location | recommended edit.
4. **Adjustment eligibility & bars** — 245(a) entry, 245(c) exemption, 245(i) if relevant, 204(c), and any other bar: clear / needs confirmation / problem.
5. **Inadmissibility screen** — each 212(a) ground: not triggered / triggered — waiver contemplated / escalate.
6. **I-864 sufficiency** — household size, income vs. 125% FPG, evidence completeness, joint-sponsor status.
7. **Bona fide marriage assessment** — coverage table by category (strong / thin / missing), timeline read, and specific fact-honest strengthening steps with `[OBTAIN: …]` and `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: …]` items.
8. **Consistency log** — every cross-document discrepancy, with where each appears.
9. **Authorities to review** — anchors + `[CITE]` placeholders, all flagged for verification.
10. **Escalation flags** — criminal history, prior removals, EWI/245(i), possible 204(c) or fraud, J-1 residence, or strategy calls routed to the supervising attorney.
11. **Prioritized edit checklist** — Blocking → Strongly recommended → Optional, so the reviewer can work top-down.

## Guardrails

- QA for the attorney — not a filing decision, not client advice, and not a finding that the marriage is bona fide.
- Strengthen articulation of *true* facts and identify real documents to gather; never invent facts, never manufacture marriage evidence, never suggest the couple add untrue details.
- Never fabricate authority; flag every citation for verification; use placeholders when unsure.
- Don't resolve criminal or fraud bars or decide strategy — escalate to the supervising attorney.
- The I-693 medical is sealed; confirm presence and currency only, never ask to open it.
- Respect the confidentiality of the clients' immigration and financial records.
