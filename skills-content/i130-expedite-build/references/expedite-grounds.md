# Expedite grounds for a pending I-130

Every value here is a **starting point and is `[VERIFY]` until confirmed in session** against the USCIS expedite request guidance page and USCIS Policy Manual Volume 1, Part A, Chapter 5. Quote USCIS only from what was retrieved this session.

Last confirmed August 14, 2026.

---

## 1. What USCIS actually says

Five criteria. USCIS may consider expediting where a request meets one or more of:

1. **Severe financial loss to a company or person**, provided the need for urgent action is not the result of the petitioner's or applicant's failure to timely file or to respond to a request for evidence.
2. **Emergencies and urgent humanitarian situations.** USCIS frames this as a pressing or critical circumstance related to human welfare.
3. **Nonprofit organization**, as designated by the IRS, whose request is in furtherance of the cultural or social interests of the United States.
4. **U.S. government interests**, including cases identified as urgent because they involve public interest, public safety, national interest, or national security. The request must come from an authorized government representative.
5. **Clear USCIS error**, where the requestor establishes an urgent need to correct it.

Also confirmed, and all of it matters to how the letter is written:

- The decision to expedite is **within the sole discretion of USCIS**. USCIS is not required to justify a decision on an expedite request and is not required to respond.
- USCIS **generally requires documentation** to support the request.
- **Not every circumstance that fits a criterion will result in expedited processing.**
- USCIS does not consider expedite requests where **Premium Processing Service is available**. It is not available for the I-130, so an expedite request is the available route.
- USCIS asks that **only one expedite request** be made per case.
- A decision on the expedite request is **not** a decision on the underlying petition.
- **The need for employment authorization, standing alone**, does not warrant expedited treatment.
- USCIS states that a **humanitarian-based filing standing alone** does not generally qualify. The filing being family based is not itself the urgency.

## 2. KNOWN PARAPHRASE WARNING

One filed firm letter states: *"USCIS policy permits expedited processing where there is urgent humanitarian need or where a delay would cause significant prejudice, including cases involving serious medical conditions and time-sensitive immigration court proceedings."*

`significant prejudice` is **not** USCIS's language, and neither `serious medical conditions` nor `time-sensitive immigration court proceedings` appears as an enumerated criterion. The sentence characterizes USCIS policy in terms USCIS does not use. An officer who knows the criteria reads it as an overreach, and it is the one line in an otherwise strong letter that invites skepticism.

Accurate substitute, adjusted to the retrieved wording:

> USCIS considers expedited processing where a request presents an emergency or an urgent humanitarian situation, which USCIS describes as a pressing or critical circumstance related to human welfare. The Beneficiary's serious illness and ongoing treatment, documented at Exhibit A, present exactly that circumstance.

Do the same thing for every policy characterization: name the criterion in USCIS's words, then tie the facts to it. Never invent a category.

## 3. Mapping I-130 fact patterns to the criteria

### Emergencies and urgent humanitarian situations

The workhorse for I-130 expedites. Fact patterns that reach it, with the evidence each needs:

| Fact pattern | Evidence that carries it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Serious or life-threatening illness of the beneficiary | Treating provider's letter on letterhead naming the patient and date of birth, the diagnosis, the treatment regimen and its duration, the time sensitivity, and the consequence of interruption | The strongest version of this ground. The El Khoury letter is the model. Characterize the condition only as the provider does |
| Serious illness of the petitioner, where the beneficiary is the caregiver or the support | Provider's letter, plus evidence of the caregiving relationship and that no one else can provide it | Weaker if other family are present in the United States. Say so honestly |
| Terminal illness or death of a close family member | Provider's letter, hospice documentation, death certificate, obituary | Where the point is reaching a dying relative, check whether the actual relief needed is humanitarian parole or expedited consular processing rather than the I-130 |
| Elderly, disabled, or otherwise vulnerable beneficiary abroad without support | Medical or disability documentation, evidence of the living situation, evidence of who else is or is not there | The Alnimri theory. See the family unity row below |
| Armed conflict, natural disaster, or extreme living conditions where the beneficiary is | Country or region specific documentation of the conditions, evidence of the beneficiary's location and exposure | Generic country conditions are not enough. Tie the conditions to this beneficiary's actual location and situation |
| Safety concern for a specific person | Documentation of the threat, protective order, police report if one exists | Never counsel obtaining a document that would not reflect what happened |
| Age-out or CSPA exposure for a child beneficiary or derivative | Birth certificate, the arithmetic on the record, the priority date posture | Time bound and objectively verifiable, which makes it a strong ground when the math is real. Show the math |

### Family unity and coordinated processing

Used in the Alnimri letter: one parent's petition approved, the other still pending, both elderly and interdependent, so processing them apart imposes real hardship and separate NVC transfer serves no administrative purpose.

Frame this **inside the urgent humanitarian criterion**, on the interdependence and vulnerability of the specific people, and never on administrative tidiness. Administrative convenience is not a criterion. Where the only argument is that it would be easier for USCIS and NVC to handle the two together, the request is weak and the attorney should be told so.

Evidence: the approval notice or receipt notice for the paired petition, marriage certificate, and documentation of the interdependence, meaning age, medical condition, or dependence in daily living.

### Pending removal proceedings

Not an enumerated criterion. **Do not write it as one.**

It works as a framing that gives the humanitarian ground its stake: a scheduled hearing is a hard, verifiable date by which the pending petition has to be resolved for the respondent to pursue the relief actually available. Write it as time sensitivity, joined to a criterion the facts independently reach, as the El Khoury letter does by pairing the hearing date with a documented illness.

Evidence: the EOIR hearing notice showing the date and court, and the current I-130 receipt notice.

Say precisely what approval unlocks and no more. Approval of an I-130 does not confer status, does not grant relief, and does not terminate proceedings. It makes an approvable immediately available visa petition exist, which is what an adjustment application or a motion needs as its predicate. A letter that implies more is a blocking defect.

Screen first, per SKILL.md Section 2. A detained beneficiary or a final order changes the calculus and goes to the supervising attorney before anything is submitted.

### Severe financial loss to a company or person

Rarely the right ground for an I-130, and easy to overstate. Reaching it requires the loss to flow from the pending petition and not from the petitioner's own failure to file timely or to respond to an RFE. A business closing, a lost contract, a home in foreclosure, an imminent layoff, or loss of critical public benefits can qualify with documentation.

The costs the Maslat and Ramadan letters describe, consulting several attorneys to monitor the case, are real hardship but are not severe financial loss in USCIS's sense. Where those facts are all there is, plead them as hardship inside the humanitarian ground and do not label them as the financial loss criterion.

Evidence: the actual financial documents. A layoff or termination notice, a foreclosure notice, a benefits termination notice, business records, the contract. An assertion of financial strain with no document behind it does not reach this criterion.

### Clear USCIS error

Available and often overlooked. Reaching it requires both a demonstrable USCIS error and an urgent need to correct it.

Fact patterns: the petition receipted in the wrong classification, the receipt notice naming the wrong beneficiary or the wrong relationship, a fee taken and no receipt issued, a case shown as transferred to an office that does not have it, a decision issued on a record USCIS does not actually have, an approval notice never generated on an approved case.

Evidence: the notice showing the error side by side with the document showing what is correct, plus the filed copy of what the office actually submitted.

Note where the error is USCIS's, since some corrections are handled by a service request rather than an expedite request, and the letter should ask for the right thing.

### The two that essentially never apply

**Nonprofit in furtherance of cultural or social interests.** Designed for petitions filed by IRS designated nonprofits. An I-130 is filed by a natural person. Do not invoke it.

**U.S. government interests.** The request must come from an authorized government representative. An attorney for the petitioner is not one. Invoking this criterion in a letter signed by counsel is a blocking defect. Where a government agency actually is behind the urgency, the agency makes the request and the letter says who and attaches their correspondence.

## 4. The honest weakness

Every ground determination in the output carries a one sentence statement of the request's weakness. It is not pessimism, it is what the attorney needs before she signs. Common ones:

- The urgency is documented but the document is stale. A provider's letter from eight months ago does not establish current treatment.
- The urgency is real but the I-130 is the wrong lever, and approval would not change the situation.
- The hardship is genuine but is the ordinary hardship of a pending petition, which is not a criterion.
- The case is well inside posted processing time and the ground is thin.
- The strongest fact is the client's own account with no third party document behind it.
- A prior expedite request on this case was declined and nothing material has changed since.

Where the weakness is that there is no ground, say that in plain words and say what would create one.
