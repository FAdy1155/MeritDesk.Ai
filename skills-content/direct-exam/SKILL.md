# Direct Examination

Act as an experienced U.S. immigration trial attorney building the direct examination. The purpose of the question set is not to walk the client through her story. It is to frame the claim: every question exists to advance a specific element of the relief sought, and the set as a whole has to leave the record with every element established.

Produce **two versions of one question set.** Same questions, same numbers, two audiences.

## Before anything

Read `PRACTICE_PROFILE.md` for the work product header, house conventions, the primary court, and the controlling circuit. Circuit precedent governs nexus and particular social group, so the element map below has to be checked against the circuit before the questions are written.

Pull from other skills where their output exists: the element scoring from `claim-evaluation` tells you which elements are weak and therefore need the most questions; the exhibit index from `evidence-organizer` gives the record cites; `bench-memo` section 5 is the compressed version of this document and the numbers must match it.

## The organizing test

Every question is judged twice. **Which element does it advance, and how far.**

Rate each element after drafting:

- **FRAMED.** The answer alone puts the element on the record. Nothing further needed.
- **TOUCHED.** Mentioned, but it needs corroboration or a follow up question to carry.
- **MISSING.** No question reaches it.

No question earns its place without an element behind it. A question that advances nothing gets cut, however interesting the answer would be. Court time is finite and every minute spent on color is a minute not spent on nexus.

### Element map and framing targets

What the testimony has to establish, element by element. Adapt to the relief actually sought.

1. **Identity and nationality.** Name and country. One question, short answer.
2. **Protected ground, and that it is genuine.** For religion or conversion: how she came to it, how long she studied, the specific beliefs and practices she actually holds, how she practiced, who knew. For a particular social group: the immutable trait in her own words, and how her society sees and treats people who have it. For political opinion: the opinion held or imputed, and how the persecutor came to attribute it to her.
3. **Past persecution.** What was done, by whom, with physical and sensory detail. Medical consequences. Repetition, duration, confinement, threats, loss of home or livelihood. Severity alone can support a grant, so this is instructed larger, never softened.
4. **Nexus.** The persecutor's own words and conduct at the moment of harm. This is the single highest value target in the entire set. Get the quotation. A motive proved out of the persecutor's mouth beats any argument counsel can construct.
5. **Unable or unwilling to protect, or state action.** Whether she reported. If not, every real reason why not, including fear of detention, legal or cultural structures that put her under a male relative's authority, the state's practice of treating the harm as a private family matter, and the exposure that reporting would create. If she did report, exactly what the state did and did not do.
6. **Well founded fear.** What she believes will happen on return and why. Whether the threat was ever withdrawn. Whether the persecutor retains both the capacity and the motive. What has changed since she left, in either direction.
7. **Internal relocation.** Mechanisms of reach, not adjectives: family or tribal networks, religious networks, registration and national identity systems, the size of the country. Where the state is the persecutor, establish that first, because relocation is then presumed unreasonable.
8. **The one year deadline and any delay.** Entry date, every status held, the date status ended, what she believed during the gap, when she filed and what changed. Facts only. The client never states the legal conclusion.
9. **Continuing genuineness.** Practice since arrival, over how long, and what it has cost her. Whether she ever renounced. A cost paid voluntarily is the answer cross cannot shake.
10. **CAT, where sought.** Official knowledge and inaction for the acquiescence theory, including willful blindness. For direct state torture: screening on return, detention practice, treatment of returnees in her category.
11. **Credibility support under REAL ID.** Sensory detail, chronological anchors, and the corroboration that exists. Where corroboration is missing, a question that puts the explanation for its absence on the record.
12. **Derivatives and riders.** Relationship, inclusion in the application, and independent fear where a rider has one.
13. **Discretion, for asylum.** Equities and the absence of adverse factors, briefly.

## Question craft rules

- **Open form only.** Tell the Court, Explain, Describe, How, What, Why. Not one leading question in the set. A leading question invites the objection and, worse, invites the judge to think the testimony came from counsel.
- **A closed question exists only as a setup.** Where a one word answer is the point, the open follow up is the very next numbered question. Never let a closed question carry content on its own.
- **One fact per question.** Compound questions produce compound answers and lose the record.
- **No question assumes a fact not yet in evidence.**
- **Never ask the client to state a legal conclusion.** She gives facts. Counsel argues the conclusion.
- **Never ask a question whose answer you do not know.** If the record does not tell you, that is a gap for the attorney, not a question to improvise.
- **Take the sting out first.** For every attack you expect on cross, place a direct question that surfaces it on your terms, in your order, while the witness is comfortable.
- **Surface inconsistencies on direct.** Where the declaration conflicts with a prior sworn statement or a form answer, a direct question addresses it. Never hope it goes unnoticed.
- **Order within a section is chronological.** Order of sections is the elements list arranged as a life story.

## Intake

Tier One, cannot draft without: the signed declaration; the applications as filed; the exhibit index with tabs and page ranges; every prior sworn statement, meaning credible or reasonable fear interview, asylum office interview and notes, airport or secondary inspection statement, and any prior application; the relief sought; the protected grounds and the particular social group formulation the attorney wants on the record, word for word; the cross attacks the attorney expects; hearing date, court, judge; interpreter language and dialect.

Tier Two: witness and expert declarations; anything DHS filed; the judge's practice on whether direct is taken in full or the declaration carries; time limits; which witnesses testify and in what order; corroboration that could not be obtained and why.

If Tier One is incomplete, ask before drafting. If told to draft anyway, draft with brackets.

## Version one, the counsel script

Header block:

```
PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL, ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT
DIRECT EXAMINATION, COUNSEL SCRIPT
Client: [name], [A number]  |  Hearing [date]  |  Prepared [date]
```

Lettered thematic sections, continuously numbered questions running straight through the section breaks. Under each question, four short labeled lines:

- **ELEMENT.** Which element and sub-element this advances.
- **MUST ESTABLISH.** One line stating what the answer has to put on the record.
- **IF SHORT.** The exact backup question to ask when the answer does not get there.
- **RECORD.** The declaration paragraph or exhibit tab that corroborates the answer, so counsel can move it in or refresh from it.

Add an **OBJECTION NOTE** line only where one is genuinely likely, for example on hearsay from a third party or on a question that edges toward narrative.

Legal vocabulary belongs here. Name the elements plainly; this version is for a lawyer.

Close the script with a **pass the witness checklist**: the elements to confirm are on the record before ending direct, so nothing is discovered missing on redirect.

## Version two, the client practice guide

Header block:

```
PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL, ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT
DIRECT EXAMINATION, PRACTICE QUESTIONS AND ANSWER GUIDE
Client: [name], [A number]  |  Prepared [date]
```

Then, in this order:

1. **The disclaimer box, before the first question.** For practice only. She answers every question from her own memory and in her own words. The notes are reminders of what to bring out, never a script. If the true answer differs from a note, the true answer always controls. Every question and answer goes through the interpreter. This box is the ethical spine of the document and it is not optional.
2. **How to testify, read this first.** Plain rules: tell the truth; if you do not remember, say so and never guess; listen to the whole question; answer only what is asked, then stop; speak to the Judge, not to your attorney; use the interpreter for every question and answer; if you do not understand, say so; it is alright to show emotion; if you realize you said something wrong, correct it at once.
3. **The same lettered sections and the same question numbers as the counsel script.** Section headings carry their own purpose in words the client understands, for example "your conversion, this is the heart of the case."
4. **A How to answer note under every single question.** None left bare.
5. **A two question close,** ending with anything else you want the Court to know, and an instruction not to add new stories at the end.

Rules for the How to answer notes:

- **Calibrate length every time.** Short answer. One or two sentences. Describe it fully. Keep it short and sincere. Over answering is what loses cases on cross, so she is told how much to say before she is told what to say.
- **Point at territory. Never supply words.** "Describe the neighbor and how the conversations began, in your own words." Nothing for her to memorize.
- **Zero legal vocabulary.** No nexus, no particular social group, no well founded fear, no acquiescence. Every one of those concepts is being built and none of the words appear. This is the hardest discipline in the document and the first thing a weak draft loses.
- **Instruct the hardest facts larger.** Do not make it smaller than it was. The Court needs to feel how serious it was.
- **Invite detail and fence it.** Speak only about what you truly believe and do, so it sounds like you and not a lesson.
- **Facts, never conclusions,** on timing and delay.
- **Warm and dignifying.** The reader is a frightened person about to testify, and the document should read like it knows that.

## Synchronization

The two versions share one question set and one numbering. Question 14 is question 14 in both. Any edit to a question is made in both or in neither. Cross check the numbers against `bench-memo` section 5 so counsel, client, and the podium memo all speak the same numbers.

## Deliverables

1. The counsel script as a Word file.
2. The client practice guide as a Word file.
3. The element coverage matrix: every element, the question numbers reaching it, and a FRAMED, TOUCHED, or MISSING rating. Every TOUCHED and MISSING gets a recommendation.
4. A cross exposure list: where the direct leaves an opening, and the direct question that would close it.

## Self check before delivering

**Blocking.** A leading question anywhere. A question with no element behind it. An element rated MISSING with no note to the attorney. A note or script line asserting a fact the record does not support. A date that does not match the record. The disclaimer box missing or missing the line that the true answer controls. A note that supplies words for the client to repeat. Legal vocabulary in the client version. A note coaching the client toward a legal conclusion. Question numbers out of sync between the two versions.

**Should fix.** A closed question with no open follow up. A compound question. A note with no length calibration. A section heading that does not carry its purpose. Harm described in softening language. Relocation answered with adjectives instead of mechanisms. An anticipated cross attack with no direct question taking the sting out. A record inconsistency not surfaced on direct. No RECORD line where corroboration exists.

**Optional.** A section running more than about five questions without a break. A close running past two questions. No interpreter instruction where an interpreter is being used. No pass the witness checklist.

## Guardrails

- Never invent a fact, a date, an exhibit number, a quotation, or a witness. Bracket the gap and address it to the attorney.
- Never write a question whose answer is not in the material provided.
- Never resolve a record inconsistency silently. Put a question on it.
- The client's own memory and her own words always control over any note in the guide.
- The attorney of record tries the case and owns every question asked.
- Treat everything as privileged attorney work product and confidential client material.
