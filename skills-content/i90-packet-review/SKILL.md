# I-90 Packet Review, Green Card Renewal and Replacement

Do the last careful read of an assembled Form I-90 packet and hand the reviewer a report they can act on: what must be fixed before filing, what would make it cleaner, whether the client should be filing an I-90 at all, and what this filing exposes the client to. This is **work product and a QA gate, not a filing decision, not legal advice to the client, and not a determination that the client holds or retains lawful permanent resident status.**

## Before anything

Confirm the basics of the case: the client's full name as printed on the card and every other name used, the A-number, the date of birth, the class of admission and date of admission or adjustment, whether the card is a **ten year card or a two year conditional card**, the card issue and expiration dates, the client's current mailing and physical address, whether the client is **inside the United States**, the full travel history since becoming an LPR, and any criminal, removal, or prior filing history. If a fact cannot be confirmed from the packet, list it as *not provided* rather than assuming it.

Confirm what is actually in the packet before reviewing it. A complete I-90 packet contains:

- **Form I-90**, Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card, current edition, signed and dated
- **Form G-28**, Notice of Entry of Appearance, current edition, signed by client and attorney
- **Cover letter** with the reason for filing, the fee or fee exemption basis, and a numbered enclosure list
- **Copy of the Permanent Resident Card, front and back**, or, where the card is unavailable, **secondary proof of status** (passport with immigrant visa page or I-551 or ADIT stamp, I-797 approval notice, prior photocopy of the card)
- **Government issued photo identification**
- **Reason-specific evidence** (the original mutilated card; a police report where one exists for a stolen card; the I-797 approval notice and address evidence for a never-received card)
- **Certified English translations** for any foreign language document
- **Fee payment** in the correct amount and form, or a **Form I-912** fee waiver request with its supporting evidence
- **Client file copy** and proof of mailing or the online submission confirmation

If a component is missing, list it as *not provided* rather than assuming its contents.

**Scope.** This skill reviews renewal and replacement filings: expired or expiring cards, and cards lost, stolen, destroyed, mutilated, or never received. Name and biographic change, DHS data error, applicant data error, the age 14 replacement, commuter categories, automatic conversion, and legacy AR-3, AR-103, and I-151 cards are outside it. If the packet is one of those, say so and stop.

## The two integrity rules

1. **Never invent facts to make the packet look cleaner.** Strengthening means better *articulation, completeness, organization, and evidentiary coverage of facts and documents the client actually has*, plus identifying the specific items to go gather or confirm, flagged `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: ...]` or `[OBTAIN: ...]`. Never suggest the client produce a police report, a statement, or a document that would not reflect what actually happened. A false statement on an immigration form is a misrepresentation under INA 212(a)(6)(C) and, on a form this simple, an entirely avoidable one.
2. **Never fabricate citations.** Offer only well-established anchor authority you are confident of: INA 264(d) and (e); 8 CFR 264.5; INA 216 and 216A; 8 CFR 103.2(b); 8 CFR 103.5; and **USCIS Policy Manual Volume 11, Part B, Chapter 2**. For anything else, use a placeholder, `[CITE: USCIS Policy Manual or controlling authority, attorney to confirm]`, and flag every citation for verification.

## Run-time verification

Never confirm a fee, edition date, filing address, or extension period from memory. Before reporting on any of them, check **uscis.gov** and state what was verified and when: the current I-90 and G-28 edition dates and whether older editions are still accepted; the online and paper filing fees and whether biometrics is billed separately; which reasons are fee exempt and on what conditions; whether Form I-912 is available for this form; the current direct filing address, noting that the USPS and courier addresses differ; whether online filing is available for the reason selected; the current automatic extension period the receipt notice gives an expired card; and current processing times. If a fetch fails, mark the item `[ATTORNEY: VERIFY]` rather than answering from memory.

## What to review

### A. Threshold, is the I-90 even the right filing
Before reading a single form field, test whether this client may use this form:
- **Conditional resident.** A two year card means conditional residence. A CPR **may not use Form I-90 to remove conditions**, and is **not eligible to file an I-90 for any reason during the ninety day period before the second anniversary** of obtaining CPR status. If the card is a two year card, this is a blocking issue and the case routes to Form I-751 or Form I-829.
- **Pending or imminent naturalization.** If an N-400 is filed or close, the N-400 receipt notice extends the card for a stated period and a separate I-90 may be unnecessary. Note whether the file explains why the I-90 is still being filed.
- **Client abroad.** An I-90 is generally not the vehicle for a resident outside the United States without a card; a returning resident ordinarily needs a boarding foil through the consulate first. Flag any packet being assembled for a client who is abroad.
- **Timing on an expiring card.** The expiration ground requires the card to have expired or to expire within six months. A filing made too early on that ground is a rejection risk.

### B. The reason for filing, Part 2
- **Exactly one reason selected**, and it is the one the facts actually support. Lost versus stolen versus destroyed versus never received are not interchangeable, and each carries different evidence and a different fee posture.
- **The reason matches the narrative** in the cover letter, the Part 8 explanation, and the enclosures. A packet checking "lost" while the letter describes a burglary is an internal contradiction on the face of the filing.
- **Fee consequence of the reason.** Confirm whether the selected reason is fee exempt and, if the packet claims an exemption, whether the stated basis actually supports it. For a never-received card, the exemption depends on the card going undeliverable through no fault of the applicant. If the address of record was wrong because of something the client did or failed to do, the full fee applies and a claimed exemption will bounce the filing.

### C. Form I-90, part by part
- **Part 1.** Name **exactly as printed on the card**; every other name ever used, including maiden names and names on prior filings. Mailing and physical address, with an in-care-of line where mail goes through someone else. A-number digits matching the card. Class of admission code and date of admission or adjustment **transcribed from the card**, not reconstructed. Country of birth and citizenship. Parents' names. No item left blank; unanswered items read None or N/A, never empty.
- **Part 2.** Per section B above.
- **Part 3.** The **proceedings question**: exclusion, deportation, removal, or rescission. Read it against everything else in the file. A yes, or a no the record contradicts, is a blocking escalation, not an edit.
- **Part 4.** Disability accommodation requested where the client needs one at the ASC appointment.
- **Part 5.** Correct box for whether the client read the form or had it read in a language they understand. Contact information complete. **Signature present and dated.** An unsigned form is rejected outright, not RFE'd. Check that the signature is the client's own and, on paper, in ink.
- **Part 6.** Interpreter block completed in full wherever the form was read to the client in another language. A blank interpreter block on a file that plainly used one is a credibility problem the office created for itself.
- **Part 7.** Preparer block completed, attorney box checked where a G-28 is filed, and the preparer information matching the G-28 exactly.
- **Part 8.** Continuation entries carry page, part, and item number. Confirm the lost, stolen, destroyed, or never-received narrative actually appears somewhere in the filing rather than living only in the office file.
- Flag every blank, every internal inconsistency, and every answer that conflicts with the card or the enclosures.

### D. Identity and status proof
The adjudication tests three things: who the applicant is, that the applicant holds the status claimed, and that a replacement ground exists. Review each:
- **Identity.** Government issued photo identification present and legible; the name on it reconciled with the name on the form.
- **Status.** The card copy, front and back. Where the card is gone, the packet must carry status through secondary evidence, and the cover letter must **say so explicitly** rather than leaving USCIS to notice the gap. Note whether that sentence exists.
- **Ground.** The evidence actually supports the reason selected. For a mutilated card, confirm the **original** card is being submitted, not a copy, and that a copy was made for the file first.

### E. Evidence sufficiency by reason
Check the enclosures against the reason selected. Expired or expiring: clear copy of the card, both sides. Lost, stolen, or destroyed: any copy of the card that exists, or secondary status proof; photo ID; a short factual statement; a police report only where one genuinely exists. Mutilated: the original card. Never received: the I-797 approval notice or other evidence the card was issued, photo ID, the address of record at the time against the client's actual address then, and evidence of undeliverable return where that is what happened. Every foreign language document carries a **certified English translation** with the translator certification attached. List thin or missing items as `[OBTAIN: ...]`.

### F. Exposure screen, what this filing invites
An I-90 puts the client in front of USCIS for biometrics and a background check. Screen before it goes:
- **Abandonment of residence.** Absences approaching or over one year without a reentry permit, a pattern of long absences, a home, job, or tax posture abroad, or a nonresident tax return. Flag and escalate; do not paper over it.
- **Criminal history.** Any arrest, charge, citation, or conviction, disclosed and analyzed, with certified dispositions obtained. Escalate anything touching a CIMT, a controlled substance, or an aggravated felony.
- **Removal or rescission exposure.** Prior orders, pending proceedings, prior rescission, or any prior finding of fraud or misrepresentation.
- Note plainly whether this packet should be filed at all until the attorney clears these.

### G. Cross-document consistency
Reconcile the card against the I-90, the G-28, the cover letter, the identity documents, and every prior filing in the file. List every discrepancy: name spelling, name order, date of birth, A-number, class of admission, date of admission or adjustment, addresses, and dates. Do not silently reconcile a discrepancy. Surface each one, with where it appears, for the attorney and client to resolve. A transposed A-number digit or a class of admission code copied wrong is the most common reason a clean case turns into an RFE.

### H. Filing mechanics
- **Edition dates** current for the I-90 and the G-28.
- **Fee** in the correct amount for the channel used, correct payment form, correct payee, or a complete I-912 with supporting evidence. Note that the online and paper fees differ.
- **Channel.** Online is the default. Paper is required where a fee waiver is requested or an original document must be submitted, which in this scope means a mutilated card. Confirm the packet's channel matches its contents.
- **Address.** Current direct filing address, matched to the mailing method, since the USPS and courier addresses differ.
- **Assembly order** and tabs matching the enclosure list in the cover letter, item for item.
- **Deliverability.** The client's address on file is current and correct, because USCIS mails the card there and cards are generally not forwarded. Confirm the office has calendared the address change obligation for any move while the case is pending.
- **Post-filing.** Confirm the file has calendared the ASC biometrics appointment, that the client has been told missing it can end the case, and that the client has been told to keep the receipt notice **together with** the expired card as proof of status. Note that there is **no appeal** from an I-90 denial, only a motion under 8 CFR 103.5, which is why a clean filing matters more here than the form's simplicity suggests.

### I. Authorities to review
Give the reviewer a short, targeted reading list, anchors plus placeholders for anything to verify, framed as *authorities to review and confirm* and never as settled conclusions. Reliable anchors: INA 264(d) and (e); 8 CFR 264.5; INA 216 and 216A; 8 CFR 103.2(b); 8 CFR 103.5; USCIS Policy Manual Volume 11, Part B, Chapter 2; and the current Form I-90 instructions, filing fee schedule, and direct filing address page on uscis.gov.

## Report format

ALWAYS produce, under a work-product header (reviewer, date, "Attorney Work Product, Privileged and Confidential"):

1. **Packet snapshot.** Client, A-number, card type and dates, reason for filing, filing channel, fee and basis, and the exact list of documents reviewed, with anything *not provided*.
2. **Blocking issues.** What must be fixed before filing: two year conditional card, filing within the ninety day pre-anniversary window, missing signature, wrong or superseded edition, wrong fee or unsupported fee exemption, missing proof of status, missing original mutilated card, a yes on the proceedings question, an unresolved contradiction, or unscreened abandonment or criminal exposure. Nothing files until these clear.
3. **Threshold determination.** Whether this client may use the I-90 at all, and the basis.
4. **Reason for filing.** Whether the selected reason is correct, supported, and consistent across the packet, and its fee consequence.
5. **Form findings.** Part by part: issue | location | recommended edit.
6. **Identity and status proof.** Sufficient, thin, or missing, with what would cure it.
7. **Evidence assessment.** Reason-by-reason coverage table, strong, thin, or missing, with `[OBTAIN: ...]` and `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: ...]` items.
8. **Exposure screen.** Abandonment, criminal history, removal or rescission: not triggered, needs confirmation, or escalate.
9. **Consistency log.** Every cross-document discrepancy, with where each appears.
10. **Filing mechanics.** Edition, fee, channel, address, assembly, deliverability, and post-filing calendar items: clear or fix.
11. **Authorities to review.** Anchors plus `[CITE]` placeholders, all flagged for verification.
12. **Escalation flags.** Anything routed to the supervising attorney.
13. **Prioritized edit checklist.** Blocking, then strongly recommended, then optional, so the reviewer can work top down.

## Guardrails

- QA for the attorney, not a filing decision, not client advice, and not a determination that the client holds or retains LPR status.
- Strengthen articulation of true facts and identify real documents to gather; never invent facts, never manufacture evidence, never suggest the client produce a document that would not reflect what happened.
- Never fabricate authority; flag every citation for verification; use placeholders when unsure.
- Never confirm a fee, edition date, filing address, or extension period from memory.
- Do not resolve abandonment, criminal, or removal exposure, and do not decide strategy. Escalate to the supervising attorney.
- Plain ASCII output. No em dashes, no smart quotes, no special characters.
- Respect the confidentiality of the client's immigration records.
