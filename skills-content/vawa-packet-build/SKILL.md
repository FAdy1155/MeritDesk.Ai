# VAWA Packet Build (Abused Spouse of a USC or LPR)

Take everything the attorney has for a VAWA client (intake notes, interview transcript, client narrative, police or medical records, protective orders, identity documents) and assemble a complete, filing-ready self-petition packet in the firm's house style: the declaration, Form I-360, Form I-485, Form I-765, the evidence plan, the affidavit templates, the cover letter, and the filing checklist. Then run the QA gate over the finished product.

Scope: abused spouse of a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident, including intended spouse, the non-abused spouse whose child was abused, and derivative children listed on the I-360. If the case is an abused child or an abused parent self-petition, say so and stop; the element tests differ and this skill is not built for them.

The self-petitioner may be of any gender. This office files for male and female self-petitioners. Use the client's actual name and pronouns throughout and never carry gendered language over from a prior client's template.

This is attorney work product and a drafting aid. It is not a filing decision and not advice to the client. The supervising attorney resolves every flagged item before anything is filed.

## SECTION 0. HARD RULES

These never bend.

1. **Never invent a fact.** Building a strong case means better articulation, completeness, organization, and element coverage of facts the client actually gave, plus identifying the specific facts still to gather. Where the narrative has a gap, a missing date, or an ambiguity, insert a bracketed flag: `[CLARIFY WITH CLIENT: approximate month of the fire?]` or `[CONFIRM WITH CLIENT IF TRUE: he kept his clothes and received his mail at the Jackson Street apartment]`. Never suggest the client add a detail because it would help. Suggesting untrue facts is suborning fabrication and destroys the case and the license.
2. **Never invent evidence.** Do not write a cover letter enclosure line for a document that does not exist. Wanted-but-absent documents go on the evidence request list, not the enclosure list.
3. **Never invent a citation.** Use only the authority in Section 3 of this skill or authority retrieved and read in this session. Anything else gets `[VERIFY CITE]`.
4. **Never put legal conclusions in the client's mouth.** The client does not say "extreme cruelty," "coercive control," "nexus," or "pattern of abuse." The client says what the abuser did and what the client felt. The legal characterization belongs in the cover letter or legal memo, not the declaration.
5. **8 U.S.C. 1367 confidentiality.** Never recommend obtaining anything from the abuser, the abuser's family, or the abuser's associates. Use a safe mailing address. Never draft a service step, a cover letter line, or a return address that would reach the abuser. Never suggest the client contact the abuser for a document.
6. **Safety first.** If the material shows current danger, open the output with a `SAFETY FLAG` block before anything else, and note that a safety plan and a protective order discussion come before packet assembly.
7. **Trauma-informed drafting.** Do not manufacture precision the client cannot support. "Sometime in the spring of 2022, I think around April" is credible; a fabricated exact date is a credibility landmine at an interview. Where memory is genuinely fragmented, say so in the client's voice and explain why.
8. **Plain ASCII output.** No em dashes, no smart quotes, no special characters, in any deliverable.
9. **Verify time-sensitive items at run time.** See Section 2. Never state a form edition, fee, filing address, or Visa Bulletin cutoff from memory.

## SECTION 1. INTAKE, POSTURE, AND MATERIAL INVENTORY

Do this before drafting a single sentence. Post it at the top of the output.

**Inventory.** List exactly what was provided, by document. List what a complete packet for this posture needs and was NOT provided. Never assume the contents of a document you were not given.

**Posture determination.** State each of these, or flag it as unknown:

- Abuser's status: USC, LPR, or U.S. national (a U.S. national is treated as an LPR for this purpose)
- Marriage posture: intact, separated, divorced before filing, divorced after filing, abuser deceased, intended spouse (abuser's prior marriage never terminated)
- Date and place of marriage; each prior marriage of the client and its termination date
- Whether the client and the abuser resided together **during the marriage**, with addresses and dates (this is now dispositive; see Section 3.6)
- Client's location: inside the United States or abroad (filing from abroad adds a requirement; Section 3.9)
- Client's immigration status and full entry history
- Removal posture: never in proceedings, in proceedings, prior order, detained
- Whether the marriage took place while the client was in removal proceedings (triggers INA 204(g) and the bona fide marriage exemption request; Section 3.5)
- Criminal or arrest history, and any prior immigration filings including any I-130 filed by the abuser or anyone else
- Derivative children: name, date of birth, marital status, age at intended filing date, location
- Whether a protective order, police report, shelter record, medical record, or psychological evaluation exists
- Which forms the attorney wants built: I-360 alone, or I-360 plus I-485 plus I-765
- Safe mailing address on file

**Four build-changing determinations.** Resolve these explicitly and say what turns on them:

1. **Can an I-485 be filed at all?** USC abuser: immediate relative under INA 201(b)(2)(A)(i), immediately available, concurrent filing available. LPR abuser: F2A under INA 203(a)(2)(A), and the priority date must be available. If the abuser is an LPR and F2A is not current, an I-485 filed now is premature. Build the I-360 alone and calendar the Visa Bulletin.
2. **Was the marriage terminated before filing?** If yes, the statutory exception must be pleaded as its own section of the cover letter and its own declaration passage. See Section 3.4. A divorced-before-filing packet that never mentions the exception is the most common fatal omission in this posture.
3. **Is this an intended spouse case?** If the abuser's prior marriage was never legally terminated, the case is not dead. It pleads as intended spouse. Do not abandon it and do not paper over it.
4. **Is there a good moral character bar in the record?** If yes, the INA 204(a)(1)(C) exception must be built into the packet from the start, in writing, all three steps (Section 3.8). It is not an afterthought.

If a determination cannot be made from the material, put it in a **client and attorney question list** at the top of the output and build around the gap rather than guessing.

## SECTION 2. RUN-TIME VERIFICATION

Before building forms, confirm against **uscis.gov** and state what you verified and on what date:

- Current **edition dates** for Forms I-360, I-485, I-765, and G-28, and whether older editions are still accepted. A superseded edition gets rejected.
- Current **direct filing address** for a VAWA I-360, and for a VAWA-based I-485 and I-765, from the page "Filing Addresses for Certain Forms Filed in Connection With a VAWA, T, or U Visa Application/Petition." **The courier address and the USPS address are different.** Confirm which mailing method the office will use and put the matching address in the cover letter block.
- Current **fees and fee exemptions or waivers** for each form, and whether Form I-912 is needed.
- **Visa Bulletin** F2A cutoff if the abuser is an LPR.
- Whether **USCIS Policy Manual Volume 3, Part D** has been amended since the December 22, 2025 rewrite (Policy Alert PA-2025-33), and the current posture on **interviews, biometrics, and RFE practice** for VAWA self-petitions.
- Whether any litigation has vacated, enjoined, or modified PA-2025-33 or any part of Volume 3, Part D. This matters: the December 2025 guidance narrowed several standards, and if a court has set part of it aside, build to the statute and regulation instead.
- Whether the **prima facie determination** notice practice and the **(c)(31)** employment authorization filing trigger have changed.
- Whether **Form I-864W** is still discontinued. As of the last check it was withdrawn and the exemption is requested in Part 3 of Form I-485. Do not include an I-864W unless USCIS has reinstated it.

If a fetch fails, say so and mark the item `[ATTORNEY: VERIFY]`. Do not substitute memory.

**Build to heightened scrutiny.** Assume the case will be interviewed, biometrics will be taken, DHS databases will be cross-checked against every answer, and an RFE is likely. Build for specificity, internal consistency, corroboration where obtainable, and affirmatively explained gaps. Length is not strength.

## SECTION 3. THE STANDARD TO BUILD TO

Current as of the December 22, 2025 rewrite of USCIS Policy Manual Volume 3, Part D. Re-verify per Section 2.

### 3.1 Burden and standard

- Preponderance of the evidence. INA 204(a); 8 CFR 204.1(a)(3); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1); 3 USCIS-PM D.2(A).
- Filed on Form I-360. 8 CFR 204.1(a)(3).
- Eligibility must be established at filing and **maintained through final adjudication** of both the I-360 and the I-485. 8 CFR 103.2(b)(1). Post-approval conduct can produce revocation. 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii); revocation appeal is 15 days, 8 CFR 205.2(d).
- If eligibility is established, USCIS lacks authority to deny in the sole exercise of discretion. 3 USCIS-PM D.5(D)(1). Build to eligibility, not to sympathy.

### 3.2 The any credible evidence rule, and what changed

- USCIS **must** consider any credible evidence relevant to the petition. INA 204(a)(1)(J); 8 CFR 103.2(b)(2)(iii); 8 CFR 204.1(f)(1); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(i). The client **may, but need not**, show that primary or secondary evidence is unavailable. 8 CFR 204.1(f)(1).
- The weight and credibility of evidence is within USCIS's sole discretion. INA 204(a)(1)(J); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(i).
- **What the December 2025 rewrite changed in practice.** USCIS moved away from a survivor-centered reading of the standard. It now emphasizes comparative weight, cross-platform consistency, and cross-checking submissions against DHS databases, and it signals that a declaration unsupported by hospital records, police reports, or social worker or case worker records may carry less weight. Officers are told to apply commonsense judgment to weight and credibility.
- **Build consequence.** The statute and regulation still forbid denial for failure to submit a particular document type, and practitioners should argue from the statute and regulation rather than the policy manual where the manual narrows the standard. But build as if corroboration will be demanded: seek every independently obtainable record, and where a record does not exist or cannot be safely obtained, **explain the absence affirmatively in the declaration** so silence does not become an adverse inference.

### 3.3 The elements

Abuser is a **USC**: INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii). Abuser is an **LPR**: INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii). Regulation: 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i).

| # | Element | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Qualifying relationship: legally valid marriage to the abusive USC or LPR at filing, or intended spouse, or a qualifying divorce or death exception | INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(aa); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(aa); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i) |
| 2 | Abuser was a USC or LPR | INA 204(a)(1); 8 CFR 103.2(b)(1) |
| 3 | Marriage entered in good faith and bona fide at inception, not to evade the immigration laws | INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(I); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i)(H); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(ix) |
| 4 | Eligible for immigrant classification | INA 201(b)(2)(A)(i); INA 203(a)(2)(A); INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(cc); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(cc) |
| 5 | Battery or extreme cruelty during the qualifying marriage, against the client or the client's child | INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I)(bb); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(I)(bb); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i)(E); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vi) |
| 6 | Resided with the abuser **during the qualifying relationship** | INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(dd); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(dd); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i)(D); INA 101(a)(33) |
| 7 | Good moral character | INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(bb); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(bb); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii); 8 CFR 316.10(a)(2) |

**Not elements.** Do not plead these; a packet that argues them signals an outdated template. Removed by the Victims of Trafficking and Violence Protection Act of 2000, Pub. L. 106-386, 114 Stat. 1464 (Oct. 28, 2000):

- **Extreme hardship** is not an element.
- **U.S. residence at filing** is not required (8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(v) superseded).
- The shared residence need not have been in the United States.
- There is no minimum duration of shared residence, and the client need not be living with the abuser at filing.

### 3.4 Element 1: qualifying relationship

- Validity by the law of the place of celebration, unless contrary to U.S. public policy. Matter of Lovo-Lara, 23 I&N Dec. 746 (BIA 2005); Matter of Da Silva, 15 I&N Dec. 778 (BIA 1976); Matter of H-, 9 I&N Dec. 640 (BIA 1962) (polygamous marriages not recognized). Common law marriage may qualify. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(B).
- **The client's** prior marriages must be shown legally terminated. 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(ii). This is not optional and is a frequent rejection point.
- **Read every divorce decree for an unexpired waiting or revocable period** (a domestic nisi period, a foreign idda period). An unexpired period means the prior marriage was not legally terminated on the date the client remarried, which can invalidate the qualifying marriage. This is one of the most commonly missed fatal problems.
- **The abuser's** prior marriages: submit termination evidence if available; absence is not fatal. If the abuser's prior marriage was never terminated, plead **intended spouse**, four showings, INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(aa)(BB) and INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(aa)(BB): (1) the client believed a legal marriage was created with a USC or LPR who was free to marry; (2) a marriage ceremony was actually performed; (3) the bona fide marriage requirements were otherwise met; (4) the apparent marriage is illegitimate solely because of the abuser's preexisting marriage. The inquiry is into **the client's** intent, not the abuser's.
- **Marriage ended before filing.** This requires the statutory exception at INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(aa)(CC) or INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(aa)(CC): filing within the statutory window after termination, **and** a connection between the legal termination of the marriage and the battery or extreme cruelty. Retrieve the exact subclause and 3 USCIS-PM D.3 and build to the actual window and connection requirement. Do not state the deadline from memory. **Draft the exception as a named section of the cover letter and a matching declaration passage.** The connection is usually available on the existing facts (the abuse caused the separation, the final incident led to the divorce filing) but it has to be written out. A divorced-before-filing packet that never mentions the exception is the most common fatal omission in this posture.
- **Divorce after filing is harmless.** INA 204(a)(1)(A)(vi); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(v). Same for the abuser's post-filing loss of status.
- **Spouse whose child was abused.** The abused child need not be the abuser's child. Include evidence of the client's relationship to that child in addition to the marital relationship. 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(ii).

### 3.5 Elements 2, 4, and 8: status, classification, and the bars

- **Primary evidence of USC status**, 8 CFR 204.1(g)(1): U.S. birth certificate from a civil authority; unexpired U.S. passport initially issued for a full 10-year period (abuser over 18 at issuance) or full 5-year period (under 18); consular officer's certification; Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship; FS-240. Other accepted: receipt or approval notice of an immediate-relative I-130 the abuser filed; the abuser's A-number with evidence of naturalization; a marriage license showing the abuser's U.S. birth.
- **LPR status**: copy of Form I-551 or other DHS proof; passport pages with visas and entry stamps; A-number.
- **The fallback to build in when no status document exists.** Supply the abuser's identifying information (full name, date and place of birth, country of birth, Social Security number, A-number if known) so USCIS can run a DHS records search. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(B); 8 CFR 103.2(b)(17)(ii); 8 CFR 204.1(g)(3). If USCIS cannot identify a record, it adjudicates on the information the self-petitioner submitted. **Never leave this out when the client has no status document.** It is the single easiest defect to cure and one of the most commonly missed.
- **INA 204(c)**: if there is any prior marriage-fraud finding, or a prior I-130 denial or withdrawal, address it head on. 3 USCIS-PM D.3(C). Unaddressed 204(c) exposure sinks the petition.
- **INA 204(g)**: if the marriage took place while the client was in removal proceedings, build a **bona fide marriage exemption request** into the packet. Without it the two-year foreign residence bar applies. 3 USCIS-PM D.3(C)(1).
- **Pending I-485 abeyance.** If the client has an I-485 pending on another basis and intends to file the I-360, USCIS recognizes a request to hold the I-485 in abeyance, made verbally in person or in writing to the field office, containing the client's name, A-number, a safe contact address, and a physical address for interview scheduling. There are 30 days from USCIS's receipt of the notification to file the self-petition. 3 USCIS-PM D.5(E). Calendar it.

### 3.6 Element 6: residence with the abuser (changed in December 2025)

- "Residence" means general place of abode or principal, actual dwelling place, **without regard to intent**. INA 101(a)(33); 3 USCIS-PM D.2(F).
- **The December 2025 rewrite reverted the February 2022 rule.** The self-petitioner must now show residence with the abuser **during the qualifying relationship**. Residence only before the marriage or only after it ended no longer satisfies the element. INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(dd); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(II)(dd); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(i)(D). (The February 2022 position rested on Dartora v. United States, No. 4:20-cv-05161-SMJ (E.D. Wash. June 7, 2021); Bait It v. McAleenan, 410 F. Supp. 3d 874 (N.D. Ill. 2019); Hollingsworth v. Zuchowski, 437 F. Supp. 3d 1231 (S.D. Fla. 2020). If the only shared residence falls outside the marriage, preserve the statutory argument in the cover letter and flag it for the attorney as a litigation-risk posture, but do not present it as settled.)
- No minimum duration; need not be in the United States; need not be current at filing.
- **The insufficiency rule to build against.** "A self-petitioner cannot meet the residency requirement by merely visiting the abuser's home while maintaining a general place of abode or a principal dwelling place elsewhere." 61 FR 13061, 13065 (Mar. 26, 1996).
- **Build consequence. This element gets its own declaration section, every time.** It must contain facts distinguishing residing from visiting: the address and dates, whose name was on the lease, where the client kept clothes and belongings, where the client slept, where mail and packages went, who else lived there, and the affirmative fact that the client had no other principal home during that period. "I stayed with him" or "our leased home" is not enough. Also build the **residence table** described in Section 4, with the marriage date overlaid so the overlap is visible on one page.
- **Caption the residence exhibits for both purposes.** A joint lease, a joint utility bill, or a joint insurance policy proves residence **and** good faith. Cover letter enclosure lines that tag them only as good-faith evidence leave the residence element looking unsupported.
- Evidence categories, 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(iii): leases, deeds, mortgages, rental agreements naming both; insurance policies with a common address; utility invoices with a common address; bank or financial statements with a common address; tax filings listing both; school records showing parent and address of record; medical records or a physician's statement; affidavits from friends and family verifying they resided together during the marriage; any other credible evidence.

### 3.7 Element 5: battery or extreme cruelty (narrowed in December 2025)

- **Battery** generally includes any offensive touching or use of force without consent: punching, slapping, spitting, biting, kicking, choking, kidnapping, rape, molestation, forced prostitution, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation, and acts that do not initially appear violent but are part of an overall pattern of violence. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E). USCIS also imported a dictionary sense of "batter" emphasizing repeated or violent blows. Where any physical contact exists, plead it explicitly and concretely in its own paragraph. Battery is the stronger theory under the narrowed standard because it does not require proof that non-physical conduct was sufficiently extreme.
- **Never assert physical abuse in a summary line and then fail to describe an incident.** A declaration that recites "emotional, verbal, physical, and psychological abuse" and then describes nothing physical loses the battery argument and hands the adjudicator an internal inconsistency. Either develop the physical incidents or remove the word.
- **Extreme cruelty** is "a non-physical act of violence or threat of violence demonstrating a pattern or intent on the part of the U.S. citizen or LPR to attain compliance from or control over the self-petitioner," case by case, no single factor conclusive. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E).
- **The December 2025 narrowing.** USCIS shifted from the abuser's intent and the impact on the particular survivor toward the objective severity of the conduct and whether it was physical, relying on dictionary definitions of "extreme" (existing in the utmost possible degree; exceedingly great or intense) to conclude that hurtful conduct alone, or adverse interactions of limited severity, do not establish extreme cruelty. Officers apply their own commonsense judgment to whether conduct is sufficiently extreme.
- **The governing test to write to.** Hernandez v. Ashcroft, 345 F.3d 824, 840 (9th Cir. 2003): non-physical acts qualify when "tactics of control are intertwined with the threat of harm in order to maintain the perpetrator's dominance through fear," because "every insult or unhealthy interaction in a relationship does not rise to the level of domestic violence" and Congress required extreme cruelty "to ensure that [the statute] protected against the extreme concept of domestic violence, rather than mere unkindness."
- **Build consequence, the three-part test.** Every non-physical allegation must carry all three of: the **control tactic** (what the abuser was extracting or preventing), the **threat or fear** it operated through, and the **severity or repetition** that makes it extreme rather than unkind. An allegation that reads as "she was mean to me" or "he refused to contribute financially" adds nothing on its own and dilutes the strong material. Develop it or cut it. A case resting on a single non-physical incident will not carry the element.
- **Rank the allegations before drafting.** Allegations that carry weight under the new standard: threats involving police or immigration authorities, retained keys or access to the home after separation, surveillance, threats about children, denial of medical care, destruction of livelihood, xenophobic degradation, and any conduct that produced documented injury. Allegations that need heavy development or should be cut: general insults without frequency or witnesses, refusal to work, ordinary financial disagreement, and characterizations of the abuser's personality with no incident attached.
- **Argue from the regulation, not only the manual.** 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vi) is broader than the December 2025 manual language and includes: any act or threatened act of violence including forced detention, resulting or threatening to result in physical or mental injury; psychological or sexual abuse or exploitation including rape, molestation, incest, forced prostitution; acts that do not initially appear violent but are part of an overall pattern of violence; acts aimed at another person or thing if deliberately used to perpetrate extreme cruelty against the client or the client's child; acts by a third party where the USC or LPR **acquiesced to, condoned, or participated in** the abuse; and spousal abuse witnessed by the victim's child as a basis for that child's own self-petition. Where the manual narrows the regulation, cite the regulation.
- **Coercive-control categories the manual lists**, to organize the declaration: isolation; humiliation; degradation, guilt, minimizing, blaming; economic control; coercion; threatening violence toward the client or the children; acts intended to create fear, compliance, or submission; controlling what the client does and who the client sees and talks to; denying access to food, family, or medical treatment; threats of deportation; threats to remove a child from custody.
- **Third-party and in-law abuse** requires pleading the abuser's own knowledge, presence, encouragement, or failure to intervene. 61 FR 13061, 13065. A recitation of in-law conduct with no link to the abuser does not establish the element.
- **Timing.** The abuse must have occurred **during the qualifying marriage**. Abuse at other times may be offered to show a pattern, and must be framed that way, not as the qualifying abuse.
- Evidence categories, 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(iv): reports and affidavits from police, judges, or court officials; court records; reports and affidavits from medical personnel; medical records; reports and affidavits from school officials; affidavit from a religious authority; reports and affidavits from social workers or social service personnel; documentation of seeking safe haven or services from a shelter or service provider; protection orders; photographs of injuries; psychological evaluations; any other credible evidence. **If a protective order exists, it goes in the packet.**

### 3.8 Element 7: good moral character

- **Evidentiary window: the 3 years immediately preceding filing**, evaluated under INA 101(f) and the standards of the average citizen in the community. 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii); 8 CFR 316.10(a)(2). Not a ceiling: USCIS may request evidence from any period if it has reason to believe GMC is lacking, and more serious past misconduct requires a longer period of good conduct. Matter of P-, 8 I&N Dec. 167 (R.C. 1958); DeLucia v. INS, 370 F.2d 305 (7th Cir. 1966); Matter of Sanchez-Linn, 20 I&N Dec. 362 (BIA 1991).
- GMC must be maintained through final adjudication of both the I-360 and the I-485. **USCIS will schedule biometrics at its discretion** and will use the results to check GMC before adjudicating either the self-petition or the adjustment application, and will deny or revoke on an adverse result. 8 CFR 103.2(b)(9); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii).
- **Disclosure discipline.** USCIS has said it will weigh a failure to disclose relevant criminal history against the credibility of the whole submission. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)(3). Every arrest, citation, and charge goes on the forms, disposition or not. Do not let the client omit anything, and do not draft around an omission.
- **Permanent bars**, INA 101(f)(8) and (9), as USCIS now frames them: aggravated felony conviction on or after November 29, 1990; conduct under INA 212(a)(3)(E) (Nazi persecution, genocide, torture, extrajudicial killing) or INA 212(a)(2)(G) (severe violations of religious freedom). USCIS treats these as bars notwithstanding the INA 204(a)(1)(C) exception; if the client has an aggravated felony, flag the conflict with the statute for the attorney rather than assuming either way.
- **Conditional bars** from acts in the 3-year window, INA 101(f)(1) through (7); 61 FR 13061, 13066: habitual drunkard; prostitution in the past 10 years; alien smuggling; practicing polygamist; CIMT conviction or admission other than a purely political offense, subject to the petty-offense and under-18 exceptions in INA 212(a)(2)(A)(ii); two or more offenses with an aggregate sentence actually imposed of 5 years or more; controlled substance violations except simple possession of 30 grams or less of marijuana; income principally from illegal gambling or two or more gambling convictions; false testimony to obtain an immigration benefit; confinement for an aggregate of 180 days or more.
- **The INA 204(a)(1)(C) exception, three steps to write out in full** (3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)(4)):
  1. **Waiver availability.** Evidence that a waiver would be **available**, not that it would be granted. Relevant waivers include INA 212(h)(1), 212(i)(1), 237(a)(7), 237(a)(1)(H)(ii).
  2. **Connection.** A **causal or logical relationship** between the act or conviction and the abuse. Da Silva v. Attorney General, 948 F.3d 629 (3d Cir. 2020). Compulsion and coercion are not required. Address the circumstances of the act and its connection to the abuse in the declaration in the client's voice, and in the legal memo as argument. The act need not have occurred during the marriage.
  3. **Discretion.** Acknowledge that USCIS may still find the severity or gravity of the act warrants a lack-of-GMC finding, and give it reasons not to.
- **Unlawful acts outside 101(f).** Willful failure to support dependents, or unlawful acts adversely reflecting on moral character: the client is considered to lack GMC **unless extenuating circumstances are established**. 8 CFR 204.2(c)(1)(vii). Never leave this silent.
- **Coerced conduct** such as forced prostitution may still permit a GMC finding **if there was no conviction**. Matter of M-, 7 I&N Dec. 251 (BIA 1956); 61 FR 13061, 13066.
- **No looking behind a conviction.** 61 FR 13061, 13066; Pablo v. INS, 72 F.3d 110, 113 (9th Cir. 1995); Gouveia v. INS, 980 F.2d 814, 817 (1st Cir. 1992). Do not build an argument of factual innocence behind a conviction; redirect to the 204(a)(1)(C) exception.
- **Catch-all.** INA 101(f): a person outside the enumerated classes may still be found to lack good moral character for other reasons.
- **Evidence to assemble**, 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(v):
  - **The client's own affidavit** is primary evidence, with detailed statements about conduct and behavior.
  - **Police clearances from each locality or state in the United States where the client resided 6 or more months during the 3 years before filing.** Build the clearance list directly from the I-360 address history so the two match. A state check from one state does not cover a second state the client lived in. Add foreign clearances for foreign residence of 6-plus months in that window; check the Foreign Affairs Manual and visa reciprocity pages for availability. Name-based checks must cover **all aliases including maiden names and every spelling variant**. If a clearance is unavailable, draft the explanatory statement.
  - **If the client has never been arrested, include an express never-arrested attestation in the declaration**, even where clearances are submitted. Officers may not deny for failure to submit clearances where the self-petitioner has submitted an affidavit attesting to never having been arrested. One sentence, and it protects the whole GMC showing if a clearance later proves unobtainable.
  - **Character affidavits** must contain the affiant's full name, address, telephone number, date and place of birth, relationship to the parties, and how the affiant acquired knowledge of the client's good moral character. USCIS has said it gives less weight to affidavits missing this information. Use the identifying block in Section 9.4 on every affidavit.
  - **With a criminal record**: arrest reports; certified court documents showing final disposition of each charge; relevant excerpts of the jurisdiction's law showing the maximum possible penalty per charge.

### 3.9 Derivatives and filing from abroad

- **Derivatives.** Unmarried children under 21 at filing may be included. INA 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(ii)(I); 8 CFR 204.2(c)(4). Same classification and priority date. INA 203(d). Children may be added later at the adjustment or immigrant visa stage without a new petition. 61 FR 13061, 13068. Age-out protection: INA 204(a)(1)(D)(i)(III). Check each child's age at the intended filing date and flag anyone within a year of 21.
- **Filing from abroad** requires additionally showing one of: the abuser is employed abroad by the U.S. government; the abuser is a member of the U.S. uniformed services stationed outside the United States; or the claimed abuse occurred in the United States. INA 204(a)(1)(A)(v); INA 204(a)(1)(B)(iv).

### 3.10 I-485 build standards

- INA 245(a). **The 245(c) bars do not apply** to a VAWA self-petitioner; INA 245(c) opens "Other than an alien having an approved petition for classification as a VAWA self-petitioner." 7 USCIS-PM B.8. Do not file a 245(i) Supplement A or argue a bar that does not apply.
- **Public charge exempt.** INA 212(a)(4)(E)(i).
- **No Form I-864 and no Form I-864W.** 8 USCIS-PM G.6. USCIS discontinued Form I-864W and removed it from uscis.gov; the exemption is requested in **Part 3 of Form I-485**. Do not include a standalone I-864W. Confirm at run time that this has not changed. (Caveat: a VAWA self-petitioner adjusting under an employment-based category may need an I-864; confirm the basis of adjustment.)
- **Visa availability** governs whether the I-485 can be filed at all. See Section 1.
- Every inadmissibility answer must match the record, and every yes needs a waiver plan in the packet.
- **If in removal proceedings**, flag: `[ATTORNEY: confirm current EOIR posture on continuances, administrative closure, and DHS's position on dismissal in this jurisdiction, and confirm jurisdiction over the I-485.]` Do not state a jurisdictional rule from memory.
- Include: two passport photos per current requirements, Form I-693 medical per current requirements (sealed civil surgeon envelope), identity and status documents, and the birth certificate with certified translation.

### 3.11 I-765 build standards

- Category **(c)(09)**, 8 CFR 274a.12(c)(9), where an I-485 is pending. This is the correct code for the standard concurrent filing with a U.S. citizen abuser.
- Category **(c)(31)**, 8 CFR 274a.12(c)(31), for an approved VAWA self-petitioner and derivative child residing in the United States, under INA 204(a)(1)(K). Use this where the I-360 is approved and no I-485 is pending, for example an LPR-abuser case waiting on F2A. Confirm at run time whether USCIS is accepting (c)(31) on an approval only, or also on a prima facie or receipt basis.
- **The wrong category code is the most common I-765 defect.** State in the output which code you used and why, tied to the actual posture and the filing sequence.
- Consider requesting a **prima facie determination** where useful; it can open access to certain public benefits while the I-360 is pending. Confirm current practice.

### 3.12 Inadmissibility screen for the I-485

Screen every ground against the record. Retrieve each cite before relying on it; mark `[VERIFY CITE]` until confirmed.

| Ground | VAWA-specific relief | Cite to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Public charge, INA 212(a)(4) | Exempt | INA 212(a)(4)(E)(i) |
| Present without admission, INA 212(a)(6)(A) | Exception where a substantial connection existed between the abuse and the unlawful entry | INA 212(a)(6)(A)(ii) |
| Unlawful presence bars, INA 212(a)(9)(B) | Battered spouse exception | INA 212(a)(9)(B)(iii)(IV) |

## SECTION 4. BUILD ORDER

Run in sequence. Each step feeds the next.

**Step 1. Eligibility triage and element scorecard.** Score all eight elements ESTABLISHED / THIN / NOT ESTABLISHED against the material provided, with the specific facts and documents supporting each. This scorecard drives what the declaration must deliberately cover and what the evidence request list must chase. Surface any bar, any 204(c) or 204(g) issue, any divorce-exception requirement, any visa availability problem, and any age-out risk now.

**Step 2. The declaration.** The center of the packet. Follow Section 5 in full and the house format in Section 9.3.

**Step 3. Evidence plan.** Two lists, kept strictly separate:
- **Enclosure list** of documents that actually exist and were provided, each tagged with the element or elements it proves.
- **Evidence request list** of documents that should be obtained, each with who obtains it, from where, how (records request, subpoena, provider letter, certified copy order), and whether it is safe to obtain given 8 U.S.C. 1367. Mark anything that cannot be obtained safely or at all, and note that the reason must be explained in the declaration.

Build three tables:

- **Residence table.** Address, move-in date, move-out date, whose name was on the lease or deed, who else lived there, corroborating document. Overlay the marriage date and the separation date so the cohabitation-during-marriage overlap is visible on one page.
- **Abuse incident table.** Approximate date, location, conduct, physical or non-physical, the control tactic, the fear or threat link, the severity or repetition, corroborating document or none.
- **Element-to-evidence matrix.** One row per element, one column per document. Any element supported by nothing but the declaration gets called out in bold.

**Step 4. Form I-360.** Produce two artifacts: a question-by-question answer table and a filled copy of the current official fillable PDF. See Section 6.

**Step 5. Form I-485.** Same two artifacts, if the posture permits filing. If it does not, say so and explain what has to happen first.

**Step 6. Form I-765.** Same two artifacts, with the category code stated and justified.

**Step 7. Cover letter and filing checklist.** Build the cover letter in the house format in Section 9.2, with the element walkthrough required by Section 9.6. Then the filing checklist: forms, editions, signatures, G-28, photographs, fees or fee waivers, translations and certifications, filing address matched to the mailing method, and copies retained.

**Step 8. Third-party declarations and affidavits.** Build the affidavit template from Section 9.4 and the list of who should provide one, with what each person can attest to from personal knowledge.

**Step 9. Legal support memo.** Optional, and worth it where the case has a real legal issue: intended spouse, divorce exception timing, a GMC bar, third-party abuse, an inadmissibility waiver, a residence timing problem under the December 2025 rule, or a non-physical-only abuse showing under the narrowed extreme cruelty standard. Argue from the statute and regulation where the policy manual narrows them, and flag every citation for verification.

**Step 10. Run the QA gate.** Run the `vawa-packet-review` skill over the assembled packet and hand the attorney that report with the packet. Then run `orthography-check` over the cover letter and declaration. Do not skip either on the theory that you just built it; the review catches cross-document inconsistencies that drafting introduces, and the orthography pass catches name spellings and template pronouns carried over from a prior client.

## SECTION 5. DECLARATION PLAYBOOK

The declaration carries the case. Under the December 2025 standards it will be read for severity, specificity, and consistency, and cross-checked against every exhibit and form. Build it accordingly.

### 5.1 Structure

House format is bold topical headings with prose paragraphs beneath them, in the client's own register. Add sequential paragraph numbers so the cover letter and any RFE response can cite directly to a paragraph. Sections, in order:

1. **Opening and personal background.** Name, date and place of birth, current safe city, date and place of marriage, date of separation and divorce if any, and a one-paragraph statement of what the client endured. Keep the background short.
2. **How we met and our courtship.** How they met, who introduced them, the date or approximate date, how the relationship developed, visits and their dates, what they did together, who each met in the other's life, the engagement, what the client believed about their future, and why the client married this person. **This section is where good faith marriage is won or lost, and it is the most commonly neglected part of real declarations.** Two sentences here is a defect. Element 3 is independently dispositive and it is exactly where a fraud-focused adjudicator probes, especially where the marriage ended shortly before filing.
3. **Our wedding.** Date, place, who was present, what it was like, whether a religious and a civil ceremony both occurred, and whether photographs exist.
4. **Our home together.** Each shared address, the dates the client lived there with the abuser, whose name was on the lease or mortgage, what the client brought and kept there, where the client slept, where mail went, who else lived there, and the affirmative statement that the client had no other principal home during that time. **Tie the dates to the marriage date explicitly.** Since December 2025 this element requires cohabitation during the marriage, so the overlap must be unmistakable. This section is mandatory and it is the one most often missing.
5. **The abuse, incident by incident, in chronological order.** See 5.2. Use descriptive sub-headings that name the conduct rather than the emotion.
6. **The pattern of control.** Organized by category, each with concrete instances: isolation; economic control; immigration and deportation threats; threats involving police or authorities; threats about the children; surveillance and monitoring; degradation and humiliation; control over movement, phone, contacts, clothing, food, medical care. See 5.3.
7. **What the abuse did to me.** What changed in the client's body, sleep, work, weight, contact with family, mental health. Concrete and specific, not adjectives. If the client saw a doctor, counselor, or clergy member, say when and what happened. Tie to any psychological evaluation in the packet without contradicting it.
8. **How it ended.** Separation, the last incident, whether police were called, whether the client went to a shelter, whether a protective order was sought, whether there is a divorce case, and where the client lives now (safe address only).
9. **Why the marriage ended because of the abuse.** Required in any case where the marriage was terminated before filing. Connect the abuse to the end of the marriage in the client's own words, without legal argument.
10. **Why I am still afraid**, if the client is, and what the client fears will happen.
11. **Good moral character.** Work, taxes, children, community and religious life, and the arrest history. **If the client has never been arrested, include the express attestation: never arrested, charged, cited, or detained by any law enforcement agency in any country.** If there is a record, address each item honestly, with the circumstances and, where applicable, the connection to the abuse.
12. **Why some records do not exist.** See 5.4.
13. **Verification.** "I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct to the best of my knowledge." Signature, typed name, date. If the client is not fluent in English, add the interpreter and translator certification (8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)) and a certificate of translation.

### 5.2 The incident template

Every abuse incident gets all of these. If a piece is missing from the client's account, flag it; do not fill it in.

- **When.** Date, or an honest approximation anchored to something the client does remember ("about two weeks after our daughter's first birthday").
- **Where.** The specific room or place.
- **Who was present.** Including children, in-laws, neighbors who may have heard.
- **What the abuser said**, as close to the actual words as the client remembers, in quotation marks.
- **What the abuser did**, in concrete physical terms. Not "she became violent" but what her hands did.
- **What the client did and felt** in the moment. Physical sensation and fear, not conclusions.
- **What happened after.** Injuries, whether care was sought and if not why not, whether anyone was told, whether police came, what the abuser did the next day, what the abuser said to keep the client quiet.
- **The control link**, for non-physical incidents: what the abuser was trying to make the client do or stop doing, and what the client understood would happen if the client refused.

Three to five fully developed incidents beat twenty summarized ones. Choose the ones with the most severity and the best corroboration, develop them completely, and then describe the remainder as a pattern with dates and short specifics.

### 5.3 Writing to the narrowed extreme cruelty standard

For every non-physical allegation, check that the paragraph shows all three of:

1. The **control tactic** (what the abuser was extracting or preventing).
2. The **threat or fear** it operated through (explicit threat, prior violence the client knew the abuser was capable of, threats about status, children, or family abroad, threats to involve police, retained access to the home).
3. The **severity or repetition** that makes it extreme rather than unkind (how often, how long, what it cost the client, what the client stopped being able to do).

If a paragraph has only one of the three, either develop it or cut it. Paragraphs that read as unkindness dilute the strong material and hand the adjudicator a reason to characterize the whole showing as adverse interactions of limited severity.

Where any physical contact occurred, plead it as battery in its own paragraph with the body parts, the force, and the result. Do not bury physical acts inside a narrative of emotional abuse.

For in-law or third-party abuse, every paragraph must say where the abuser was, what the abuser knew, and what the abuser said or failed to do. Without that link the conduct does not count.

### 5.4 Gap explanation

Every category of evidence that a reasonable adjudicator would expect and that does not exist gets an affirmative explanation in the client's voice, tied to the facts:

- Why police were not called, or were called and no report was made.
- Why no hospital visit, or a visit where a different reason was given for the injury.
- Why there are no photographs.
- Why the lease is only in the abuser's name.
- Why there are no joint accounts.
- Why friends and family did not know.
- Why the client has no documents from the marital home.
- Why a record cannot be obtained now.

Silence on a gap becomes an adverse inference. An explanation converts the gap into corroboration of the control.

### 5.5 Voice and credibility rules

- The client's register, vocabulary, and sentence length. If the intake is in Spanish or Arabic, the English should read like the client, not like a brief.
- No legal terms of art in the client's mouth.
- No emotional intensifiers substituting for facts. Cut "horrific," "unbearable," "living nightmare" and replace with what happened.
- No manufactured precision. Approximations, honestly framed, are more credible than false certainty.
- Every factual assertion must trace to something the client actually said or a document actually provided. Nothing enters the declaration from the drafter's imagination.
- Nothing in the declaration may contradict any form answer, any exhibit, or the psychological evaluation. Reconcile as you draft, not at the end.
- No repeated sentences and no duplicated paragraphs. Read the finished draft straight through once for repetition before delivering.
- Collect all `[CLARIFY WITH CLIENT]` flags into a list at the end of the declaration so the attorney can run one client call.

### 5.6 Third-party declarations

Draft the template in Section 9.4 and a list of who should provide one. Each declarant must state the full identifying block, **how they know what they are saying** (what they personally saw or heard, and when), and specific facts rather than a restatement of the client's story.

Push for detail where the personal knowledge is good. A neighbor who heard the abuse through a shared wall should say which months, how often, what was heard, and whether anything was seen. A two-sentence affidavit from the best-positioned witness in the case is a wasted exhibit.

**Never draft identical or near-identical language across declarants.** Identical phrasing across affidavits reads as coached and damages credibility more than the affidavits help. Each declarant writes what that person actually witnessed.

## SECTION 6. HOW TO BUILD THE FORMS

For each of the I-360, I-485, and I-765 you deliver two artifacts: an **answer table** and a **filled PDF**. The answer table is the source of truth; the PDF is filled from it; the attorney checks one against the other.

### 6.1 Get the current blank form

USCIS revises these forms and rejects stale editions, so never rely on a remembered field layout.

1. Ask the attorney for the current blank fillable PDF they intend to file.
2. If none is provided, locate the current edition from the official USCIS form page, verify the edition date printed on the form, and note in the output which edition you used.

If you cannot obtain the fillable PDF, still produce the complete answer table and state clearly that the PDF fill is pending the current blank form. Do not fabricate a form or a field layout. Use the `pdf` skill to fill the form.

### 6.2 Fill rules

- Fill only from facts in the record. Where a required field has no supporting fact, do not guess: put `[CLARIFY WITH CLIENT: exact date and place of last entry]` in the answer table and leave the PDF field blank and marked for follow-up.
- A truthful negative gets written, not left empty. "None" and "N/A" are answers; blanks are defects.
- Every name the client has ever used, including maiden names and every spelling variant, goes in the other-names fields, and the same set goes into the police clearance requests.
- Address history complete with no unexplained gaps, and consistent across all three forms and the declaration.
- Entry history complete: every entry with date, place, and manner.
- Criminal history answered truthfully and completely on every form, including arrests without conviction.
- Interpreter part completed where the client is not fluent in English. Preparer part completed. Form G-28 signed if the attorney is appearing, and the G-28 box on the form checked with the attorney bar number and USCIS online account number.
- Signatures present and dated, and no signature predating a later-amended answer.
- Safe mailing address on every form, including the alternate and safe mailing address item on the I-360.

### 6.3 The I-360 items that are most often wrong

- **Part 2 classification box must match the abuser's status.** Box I is Self-Petitioning Spouse of Abusive U.S. Citizen or Lawful Permanent Resident. Box J is the abused child and box K is the VAWA parent. Check it every time and record in the output which box you selected.
- The abuser's identifying information section, complete, including the information needed for the DHS records search fallback where no status document exists.
- Derivative children listed with correct names and dates of birth, matching the birth certificates.
- Address history matching the police clearance set.
- All other names including maiden names.

### 6.4 The I-485 and I-765 items that are most often wrong

- I-485: inadmissibility answers inconsistent with the record; an I-864 or a standalone I-864W included where neither belongs; a 245(i) Supplement A included where 245(c) does not apply; a filing made when F2A is not current; Part 3 exemption request left blank.
- I-765: the wrong eligibility category at Item 27. State the code, (c)(09) or (c)(31), and the reason.

### 6.5 Cross-document reconciliation before delivery

Build the table and fix every mismatch before the packet goes to the attorney.

| Fact | I-360 | I-485 | I-765 | Declaration | Exhibits | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rows, at minimum: full legal name and its exact spelling; all other names, maiden names, and spelling variants; the abuser's full name and its exact spelling; date of birth; place of birth; A-number; date and place of marriage; each prior marriage of the client and its termination date; each prior marriage of the abuser if addressed; each shared address with move-in and move-out dates; date of separation; date of divorce if any; each entry to the United States with date, place, and manner; current immigration status; each child's name and date of birth; the date of each abuse incident; employment history dates; police clearance jurisdictions against the address history.

Do this exhaustively, not by sampling. Cross-document inconsistency is the most common cause of RFEs and denials, and drafting is what introduces it. Name spelling is the most common inconsistency of all: check the client's name and the abuser's name character by character against the passport, the marriage certificate, and the divorce decree.

A copy of the affiant's photo identification goes on the page immediately following. The identifying block is required by 8 CFR 204.2(c)(2)(v) and USCIS has said it gives less weight to affidavits that omit it, so no affidavit leaves the office without it.

### 9.5 Marriage-witness letter

For a witness who can only speak to the ceremony: the witness's name and address block, the date, "To Whom It May Concern," a statement of what the witness personally observed at the ceremony and that both parties were present and willingly participated, an offer to be contacted at the details above, "Sincerely," and the name. Where the witness knows more than the ceremony, use the full affidavit template in 9.4 instead; a ceremony-only letter wastes a witness who could speak to the relationship.

### 9.6 Assembly conventions

- No tab dividers and no exhibit numbers. Documents follow in the order listed in the cover letter, and the cover letter list serves as the index. Keep the two in exact correspondence: every enclosure line has a document behind it, in that order.
- Sealed civil surgeon envelope scanned and placed at the front of the packet.
- Two passport photos with each of the I-485 and the I-765.
- Affiant photo identification immediately after each affidavit.
- Psychological or psychosocial evaluation by a licensed professional, structured as a clinical report: the evaluator's credentials, a statement of whether the evaluator is the client's treating clinician and the purpose of the meeting, the number and dates of sessions, the instruments administered, DSM-5 coded diagnoses, and a reasoned summary and conclusions. Hold every evaluator to this standard; an evaluation that notes the absence of symptom over-endorsement is more persuasive than one that does not.
- Criminal history: a state or local criminal identification report for **each** jurisdiction of six months or more residence in the three-year window, plus a clearance from the country of origin, plus certified translations.
- **Trim exhibit boilerplate.** Submit the pages that prove something: the lease signature page and any page naming both parties at the shared address, the insurance declarations page, the tax transcript itself. Do not file complete policy texts, endorsement sets, privacy notices, or lease addenda about pool hours. Volume buries the two pages that carry the element and costs postage on every packet.
- Do not include Form I-864 or Form I-864W. The public charge exemption is claimed in Part 3 of the I-485.

### 9.7 Every packet gets these before it goes out

1. A dedicated home-together section in the declaration and a residence table.
2. A full courtship section in the declaration.
3. The three-part test applied to every non-physical allegation.
4. The divorce or death exception pleaded in writing if the marriage ended before filing.
5. The identifying block on every affidavit.
6. The never-arrested attestation where applicable.
7. Gap explanations for every expected record that does not exist.
8. Numbered declaration paragraphs.
9. The element walkthrough at the top of the cover letter.
10. An `orthography-check` pass over the cover letter and declaration for name spellings, pronouns, and duplicated sentences.
