# Authority Bank (Houston Immigration Court, Fifth Circuit)

These are the authorities the firm's exemplar brief uses, organized by brief section, with what each stands for. They are the citation backbone of every merits brief. Use them where the doctrine applies; do not force a citation onto facts it does not fit.

Two rules:

1. Never invent a citation. If a proposition needs authority this bank does not supply, either cite the governing statute or regulation directly, or add case law marked NEEDS VERIFICATION in the cover note. A fabricated or wrong citation in a filed brief is a sanctionable event.
2. Law moves. These authorities were good as of the exemplar (2026), but the attorney must confirm each remains good law before filing. Say this in the cover note every time.

## Section II: Refugee definition, country of removal, and timeliness

- INA 101(a)(42)(A) - refugee definition.
- 8 C.F.R. 1208.13(b)(1) - past persecution creates a rebuttable presumption of well-founded fear.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421, 431 (1987) - fear is well founded even at a ten percent chance of persecution.
- Matter of Mogharrabi, 19 I&N Dec. 439, 446 (BIA 1987) - fear established where persecutor knows of the applicant, has means to harm, and is inclined to do so.

## Section III: Particular social group

- Matter of Acosta, 19 I&N Dec. 211, 233 (BIA 1985) - immutable or fundamental characteristic.
- Matter of M-E-V-G-, 26 I&N Dec. 227 (BIA 2014) - particularity and social distinction.
- Orellana-Monson v. Holder, 685 F.3d 511, 521-22 (5th Cir. 2012) - Fifth Circuit adoption of the framework.
- Matter of W-Y-C- & H-O-B-, 27 I&N Dec. 189 (BIA 2018) - PSG must be stated with exactness before the IJ.
- Matter of L-E-A-, 28 I&N Dec. 304 (A.G. 2021) - family remains a viable PSG basis, case-by-case.
- Vazquez-Guerra v. Garland, 7 F.4th 265, 269 (5th Cir. 2021) - family-based PSG treated as cognizable, but relief denied for lack of nexus; cite for the cognizable point only, and note the nexus risk.
- CAUTION - Faddoul v. INS, 37 F.3d 185 (5th Cir. 1994) - ADVERSE. It denied asylum to a stateless Palestinian and held that statelessness alone is not persecution. Do NOT cite it as recognizing Palestinian-origin or statelessness claims. Cite only to distinguish, or not at all.
- Gonzales-Veliz v. Barr, 938 F.3d 219, 232 (5th Cir. 2019) - harm-defined groups are circular and fail.
- Jaco v. Garland, 24 F.4th 395, 404-06 (5th Cir. 2021) - same; the anti-circularity section must engage both.

## Section IV: Nexus

- INA 208(b)(1)(B)(i) - protected ground need be only one central reason.
- Matter of J-B-N- & S-M-, 24 I&N Dec. 208, 214 (BIA 2007) - mixed motives do not defeat nexus. This plus the statute carries the one-central-reason point on its own.
- CAUTION - Argueta-Hernandez v. Garland, 87 F.4th 698 (5th Cir. 2023) - VERIFY before use. A decision in this matter was a jurisdictional dismissal that never reached nexus, and the reporter cite has been unreliable. Do not cite for the one-central-reason standard without confirming the correct opinion and holding on Westlaw or Lexis; the statute and J-B-N- already supply the point.
- Matter of S-P-, 21 I&N Dec. 486, 489 (BIA 1996) - imputed political opinion.
- Ramirez-Mejia v. Lynch, 794 F.3d 485 (5th Cir. 2015) - petition DENIED; economically motivated crime lacks nexus (and a family PSG lost on nexus). Cite defensively, to DISTINGUISH, when the persecutors demanded allegiance or silence rather than money.

## Section V: Persecution and torture

- Abdel-Masieh v. INS, 73 F.3d 579, 584 (5th Cir. 1996) - harm assessed cumulatively.
- Gjetani v. Barr, 968 F.3d 393 (5th Cir. 2020) - the harassment/persecution line; use when the worst harm clearly crosses it.
- Eduard v. Ashcroft, 379 F.3d 182, 188 (5th Cir. 2004) - threats as persecution where sufficiently concrete and menacing.
- Qorane v. Barr, 919 F.3d 904, 910 (5th Cir. 2019) - same, limits.
- 8 C.F.R. 1208.18(a)(1) - torture definition.
- Matter of J-E-, 23 I&N Dec. 291, 297-98 (BIA 2002) - torture elements.

## Section VI: State action, well-founded fear, relocation, firm resettlement

- 8 C.F.R. 1208.13(b)(1)(iii) and Matter of Chen, 20 I&N Dec. 16 (BIA 1989) - humanitarian asylum from severity of past persecution.

## Section VII: Withholding and CAT

- INA 241(b)(3) and 8 C.F.R. 1208.16(b) - withholding standard and presumption.
- INS v. Stevic, 467 U.S. 407, 429-30 (1984) - more-likely-than-not standard.
- 8 C.F.R. 1208.16(c), 1208.18(a)(1), (a)(7) - CAT standard, definition, acquiescence including willful blindness.
- 8 C.F.R. 1208.16(c)(4), 1208.17 - CAT withholding and deferral.

## Section VIII: Hearing rights and pretermission

- INA 240(b)(1), 240(b)(4)(B) - right to present evidence, examination under oath.
- INA 208(b)(1)(B)(iii) - credibility factors (demeanor, candor, responsiveness).
- Matter of Fefe, 20 I&N Dec. 116, 118 (BIA 1989) - full examination of the applicant required.
- 8 C.F.R. 1240.11(c)(3) - hearing procedures for asylum claims in removal proceedings.

## Common additions (verify before use)

Claims differ. Typical doctrines that may need authority beyond this bank: one-year filing deadline and exceptions (INA 208(a)(2)(B), (D); 8 C.F.R. 1208.4); firm resettlement (8 C.F.R. 1208.15); gang recruitment claims (Fifth Circuit law is hostile; be candid with the attorney); gender-based PSGs; religious conversion claims; whistleblower/anti-corruption political opinion. For each, cite statute and regulation confidently, and mark any case law NEEDS VERIFICATION.

- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1460306/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 3, 2026, version 0.2.0
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: (1) The removal of former President Maduro from power and the ensuing transfer of executive authority constitute a change in country conditions in Venezuela for purposes of assessing an applicant's prospective fear of persecution. ### Matter of M-R-I-, 30 I&N Dec. 137 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4258
- Source: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1464646/dl?inline (listed on https://www.justice.gov/eoir/volume-30)
- Added: October 12, 2026, version 0.3.1
- Type: BIA precedent
- Text: (1) An alien who, under a grant of advance parole, returns to a country of claimed persecution has abandoned his or her application for asylum, withholding of removal, and protection under the Convention Against Torture, absent compelling reasons for the return. (2) The application of a mandatory bar to relief is not triggered by the argument of counsel but is governed by the evidentiary record.
- Check above: Section II (refugee definition and timeliness), Section VII (withholding and CAT) and Section VIII (pretermission): a return to the country of claimed persecution on advance parole is treated as abandonment of all three applications absent compelling reasons, so screen every client's travel history; headnote 2 means a mandatory bar turns on the evidentiary record, not on counsel's argument.

### Matter of B-S-A-N-, 30 I&N Dec. 145 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4259

- Text: (1) Immigration Judges should generally deny asylum applications filed by nationals of highly developed, democratic countries that broadly respect and protect human rights, except in extraordinary circumstances involving national security or foreign policy considerations. ### Gakou v. Warden of the Prairieland Detention Center, No. 26-10925 (5th Cir. October 10, 2026) (published order)
- Source: https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/26/26-10925-CV0.pdf (listed on https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions?from=2026-10-03&to=2026-10-12&group=grouped)
- Added: October 12, 2026, version 0.3.1
- Type: Court of appeals published opinion
- Weight: Binding in the Fifth Circuit, including the Houston Immigration Court.
- Text: Slip op. 2 to 3: "'A request for stay of removal is a challenge to a removal order' within the ambit of 8 U.S.C. 1252(g)'s jurisdiction-stripping effect. ... Therefore, we lack the power to entertain Gakou's motion." Slip op. 3: "As a result, Gakou's claim to an injunction against his removal to a country besides Senegal is unavailing and cannot be used to support the stay he currently seeks."
- Check above: Section II (country of removal) and Section VII (CAT): in a district court habeas case, the Fifth Circuit held it lacks jurisdiction under 8 U.S.C. 1252(g) to stay removal and rejected a bid to enjoin third-country removal pending CAT litigation, relying on the Supreme Court's September 29, 2026 order in DHS v. D.V.D., Nos. 26-426, 26A406. Raise CAT and country-of-removal objections before the Immigration Judge, not by later stay motion. Order denies an emergency motion; confirm its precedential reach before citing.
