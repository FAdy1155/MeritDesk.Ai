# House Style: EOIR Merits Brief Architecture and Formatting

This is the firm's brief architecture, extracted from the approved exemplar and updated to the firm's current house style. Every merits brief opens with a short COMES NOW paragraph (no narrative introduction section) and then uses the nine numbered sections below, in this order. Adapt the content of each section to the client's claim; do not reorder, merge, or drop sections. If a section truly has nothing to say for a given client (rare), keep the section and say in one paragraph why it is satisfied or inapplicable.

## Document front matter

**Cover page** (its own page):
- Counsel block, top left: attorney name, firm, address, phone, fax, email.
- Detention status in caps, right side: NON-DETAINED (or DETAINED).
- Full court heading, centered: UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE / EXECUTIVE OFFICE FOR IMMIGRATION REVIEW / IMMIGRATION COURT / HOUSTON, TEXAS.
- Caption: "In the Matter of:" with client name (SURNAME in caps, then given names) and "Respondent(s)", a column of section marks, then "File No.: A xxx-xxx-xxx" and "In Removal Proceedings".
- Immigration Judge name and next hearing date and time.
- Title, centered, bold, caps: RESPONDENT'S BRIEF IN SUPPORT OF APPLICATION FOR ASYLUM, WITHHOLDING OF REMOVAL, AND PROTECTION UNDER THE CONVENTION AGAINST TORTURE.

**First page of the brief** repeats the court heading, caption, and title, then the COMES NOW opening, then Section I.

## The opening (replaces the old narrative Introduction)

The firm no longer opens with a narrative "I. INTRODUCTION" section. Instead, immediately after the repeated title on the first page, the brief opens with a single short COMES NOW paragraph, unnumbered, in the ordinary body style. It states only: who is appearing (COMES NOW, SURNAME, First, Respondent, by and through undersigned counsel), the relief sought (asylum, and alternatively withholding of removal or protection under the Convention Against Torture), and the grounds and social-group formulation the claim rests on, closing with the line that each ground and each form of relief is independently sufficient. It is a plain statement of what is sought and on what basis, not a persuasive summary of the facts. Keep it to one paragraph.

Template to adapt:

> COMES NOW, [SURNAME, First], Respondent, by and through undersigned counsel, and respectfully submits this brief in support of [his/her] application for asylum, and alternatively for withholding of removal or protection under the Convention Against Torture (CAT). Respondent seeks relief based on [his/her] past persecution and well-founded fear of future persecution on account of [grounds: e.g., religion, race, nationality, political opinion], and membership in particular social groups comprising [state each PSG precisely], and under the Torture Convention. Each ground and each form of relief is independently sufficient.

Where there are multiple applicants (a lead respondent and riders), adapt to "COMES NOW, [SURNAME, First], and riders" and use "their" and "Respondent and riders."

## The nine numbered sections

**I. STATEMENT OF FACTS.** Chronological, told as an escalation in stages. Establish identity and background first (nationality, religion, ethnicity, family, livelihood), then the persecutors and their demand or motive, then each incident in order with dates, then the state's response at each point, then the harm, then the threats, then failed relocation or escape attempts, then departure and entry into the United States and placement in proceedings. Every fact the argument sections will use must appear here, and no fact may appear here that is not in the client's materials. Because there is no longer a narrative introduction, the Statement of Facts carries the full weight of telling the story; make it complete.

**II. RESPONDENT IS A REFUGEE.** The legal framework section. Refugee definition (INA 101(a)(42)(A)), presumption from past persecution (8 C.F.R. 1208.13(b)(1)), the ten percent standard (Cardoza-Fonseca), and the Mogharrabi test. Where the country of removal matters (dual nationality, statelessness, multiple possible return countries), state expressly which country the claim is assessed against and that it stands on its own on that footing. Fold the one-year timeliness analysis here where it applies. End by asserting the client meets the definition through both past persecution and independent well-founded fear.

**III. PSG COGNIZABILITY.** Heading states the conclusion, for example: "RESPONDENT'S PARTICULAR SOCIAL GROUPS ARE COGNIZABLE AND NOT CIRCULAR". Open by stating each PSG with precision. Set out the three-part test with authority. Then show each group satisfies each prong with the client's specific facts. Then the mandatory anti-circularity passage: show each group is defined by kinship, descent, status, or a completed act rather than by the harm feared, that each group would exist unchanged had the client never been harmed, and distinguish the Fifth Circuit's harm-defined-group cases expressly. A brief without this passage does not go out; circularity is the government's first attack in this circuit. If the claim rests on non-PSG grounds only, this section instead confirms those grounds and explains briefly why no PSG formulation is needed.

**IV. NEXUS.** Heading states the conclusion, for example: "A PROTECTED GROUND WAS AT LEAST ONE CENTRAL REASON FOR THE PERSECUTION". One central reason standard with mixed motives. Then the client's nexus evidence: the persecutors' own words, categorized ground by ground; the escalation timeline tracking each protected act; and the inference that the campaign's scale exceeds private grudge or ordinary crime. Then each ground independently: religion, political opinion (actual or imputed), family, origin, or the PSGs. Close by preemptively rebutting the government's recharacterization (crime, personal dispute, extortion) and distinguishing its likely authority.

**V. HARM.** Heading, for example: "THE HARM IS PERSECUTION AND CONSTITUTES TORTURE". Cumulative assessment first. Then show the worst harm crosses the persecution line even in isolation. Then threats as independent persecution where concrete and menacing. Then map the same facts to the torture definition element by element: intentional infliction of severe pain or suffering, for a proscribed purpose, with official consent or acquiescence, outside lawful sanction. Close with the a fortiori point: harm meeting the torture standard necessarily meets the persecution threshold.

**VI. STATE ACTION, WELL-FOUNDED FEAR, AND RELOCATION.** Heading states the full conclusion. Argue every state-action track the facts support: direct participation by state agents; deliberate inaction after notice (use the contrast device where the record allows: the state acted on the persecutors' behalf but not the victim's); and the discriminatory reason protection was withheld. Then: the presumption of well-founded fear cannot be rebutted and the fear is independently well founded (persecutors know him, can reach him, are inclined to harm him). Then relocation: specific facts of failed relocation, the persecutors' nationwide reach, and why no region offers different protection. Where the years-abroad facts invite it, add a firm-resettlement rebuttal under 8 C.F.R. 1208.15 (no offer of permanent status). Close with the humanitarian asylum fallback based on severity of past persecution.

**VII. WITHHOLDING AND CAT ARE INDEPENDENTLY ESTABLISHED.** Withholding: mandatory where persecution is more likely than not; past persecution raises the presumption; asylum-specific limits (one-year deadline, discretion) do not apply; a denial of asylum does not resolve withholding, which must be adjudicated on its own terms. CAT: no nexus requirement at all; requires only likelihood of torture with official consent, acquiescence, or willful blindness; all evidence of future torture weighed in the aggregate; survives complete rejection of every protected ground. Argue CAT as to each country of potential return the record supports, distinguishing direct torture by state actors (no acquiescence showing needed) from acquiescence or willful blindness (which requires official awareness plus a failure to act that exceeds mere inability). Request withholding under CAT or, in the alternative, deferral.

**VIII. PRIMA FACIE ELIGIBILITY, REQUEST FOR INDIVIDUALIZED HEARING, AND OBJECTION TO PRETERMISSION.** Taken as true, the facts establish every element, so the applications cannot be denied on the papers; any adverse resolution of fact, inference, or credibility requires the statutory hearing with live testimony. Enumerate now the issues that would require testimony if contested (persecutors' statements and motives, police conduct, relocation reasonableness, threat immediacy, future harm likelihood, credibility). Preserve the objection to pretermission. State that no single adverse finding can dispose of the applications and ask that any adverse decision address each ground and each form of relief separately on this record.

**IX. CONCLUSION.** Cascading alternative requests: asylum; in the alternative withholding under INA 241(b)(3); in the alternative CAT withholding or deferral; in the further alternative humanitarian asylum; and before any adverse determination, the individualized hearing. "Respectfully submitted," followed by the counsel block.

**CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE** (its own page, after the signature block): client name and A-number at top, service date line, method (By eService), recipient (Office of the Principal Legal Advisor - Houston, 126 Northpoint Drive, Room 2020, Houston, TX 77060), signature and date lines.

## Style rules

- Declarative, confident prose. No hedging inside the brief itself (hedges and weaknesses go in the cover note to the attorney).
- Short punch sentences close analytical paragraphs ("A death threat from those who have already maimed the person threatened is not idle."). Aim for one per major section; more feels theatrical.
- Appositives and asides set off with commas. No em dashes anywhere in the document.
- Citations inline, Bluebook form, no footnotes. Cite the exemplar hierarchy: statute, regulation, BIA, Fifth Circuit.
- Argue the opponent's case before the opponent does. Every section that can be attacked names the attack and answers it.
- No new facts after the Statement of Facts (Section I). If it matters, it goes there.
- The COMES NOW opening is a plain statement of relief and grounds, not argument or a factual summary; keep it to one paragraph and let the Statement of Facts and the numbered sections do the persuasive work.
- Length: the exemplar runs about 14 pages. Ten to sixteen pages is the target band (before front matter); shorter means underdeveloped sections, longer means padding.

## Word formatting specification (python-docx)

- Letter page size, 1 inch margins.
- Times New Roman 12 pt throughout.
- Body paragraphs double-spaced, first-line indent 0.5 inch, justified.
- Cover page and caption blocks single-spaced.
- Section headings: bold, centered, ALL CAPS, single-spaced.
- Caption layout: a 3-column table with invisible borders; left cell holds "In the Matter of:", the client name, "Respondent"; middle cell a vertical run of section marks (one per line, about 8 to 11 of them); right cell "File No.: A xxx-xxx-xxx" and "In Removal Proceedings". The section mark character is the legal section symbol.
- Page break after the cover page (so it stands alone) and before the Certificate of Service. The COMES NOW opening and Section I follow the repeated caption on the first text page.
- If a Table of Contents and Table of Authorities are included, place them on their own pages after the cover page and before the first text page.
- Do not number the cover page; body page numbers centered in the footer are acceptable but optional (the exemplar omits them).
- File name convention: "Legal Brief - [Client Name].docx".
